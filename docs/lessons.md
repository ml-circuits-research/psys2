# Lessons: how worlds are made

Standing direction (owner, 2026-11-13): accumulate experience in how worlds are made, improve sopworld, generalise, accumulate cases, learn to combine worlds.
After every landing, 3 to 5 lines: what generalised, what did not and why, which mechanism should exist in the library or the knowledge repo instead.
Newest landing first when appended; the first block below is the backlog of the landings up to 2026-11-14.

Marks: **[LIB]** = a mechanism for the sopworld library (the reviewer forwards these lines); **[KNOW]** = belongs in the knowledge repo (sop-knowledge-worlds / catalog); no mark = stays in PSys2 or is a note about method.

## Order in time, one noun-phrase resolver, referent as evidence (worker-log "Order in time, one resolver, referent evidence")
- Generalised: a question that puts two moments in order ("is Monday before Friday", "does spring come after winter", "is 3 o'clock after 5pm", a day against a month) is recognised once from the temporal preposition facts (`en.timeComparison` over `en.timeRelation` and the `lang.en.timeword` names) at the dialogue level, for every tab and both reasoners; until a world has an order in time the answer says it cannot compare yet, never "4 days" (cases h21-h23 right: 2 of 5 -> 4 of 5 temporal; the open one, "is March during the summer", needs the season facts of k.time). Two noun-phrase resolvers (the acts executor, art.draw `resolveNP`) now call ONE host function (`src/lang/np-resolve.mjs`: choice, part of a set, relative clause, "the other one"), 52 lines of duplicated logic removed from the two worlds; test-world all (both reasoners), the owner logs a / b / c, the lang holes and the coverage grid (1,689 cells) are identical row by row. A referent held by a world the user worked in lately is evidence for that world ("remove milk" on the drawing board while the list holds milk goes to the list; spec 20 rule 5), by the same whole-word lookup as rule 2, with no phrase listed; the six invariants stay green.
- Did not generalise: the two domains still look up a PLAIN phrase in their own way (kind, number, name, attribute value in the acts; kind, colour, size, place, ordinal in the drawing: 1,000 lines of parser), so "one resolver" is the shared reading plus an adapter of five functions, not one parser; the library's `resolve(phrase) -> ids + tier` (handover/lang.en/std-grammar.md section 8) is what would remove the adapters. "delete bread" on the drawing board is still guessed by best effort as "delete my bread list" (a list named bread, which exists only by the verb's other sense): the referent evidence drops the board's refusal but cannot say WHICH frame of the other world the words fill; that is `std.fill` (delegation with tiers), not another rule here.
- **[KNOW]** `k.time` has `n` on weekdays, months and seasons and `after` on seasons, but no `before / after` relation derivable by one rule, no clock hours, no parts of the day, no cyclic successor; `timeComparison` reads the names from `lang.en.timeword` facts only until it does, and `plan.timeline` cannot sort things with a temporal aspect (nothing to sort by) until it is published. `w.lang.en` is not in the catalog yet (its second worker is importing the hand-over), so `src/lang/en.mjs` still loads the hand-over (`createLangEn(source)` takes the catalog's wires when they exist).
- **[LIB]** `std.grammar` `resolve` (above); and an evidence rule for the world of a referent belongs in `std.focus` / `std.fill` (the world whose things hold the words is a candidate of the delegation, ranked by the tier "scene").
- Method: the evidence rule was written against the mechanism first (what is the tab's own state, who holds the words) and found that two guesses were wrong on the way (tab worlds: the tasks world has no tab; every world's state: the reader's sample story holds "bread"); a world is evidence only if the user has worked in it.

## data.charts switch: a data world became schema + acts (worker-log "data.charts switch")
- Generalised: one grammar read 267 of the 277 old rows with 163 lines of schema and 29 new generic forms (`worlds/acts/forms.data.mjs`: an enum after a determiner, a switch, a text value, a number said for a thing, an amount or a factor, "go to the next one", an order, data first); the hand patterns went from 365 to 163, the hand lexicon from 1,750 to 1,100 lines, `matrix.sop` / `fns.mjs` / the 40-line `interceptInput` of regexes are gone. The same forms read "hide the legend", "call it Fruit" and "pears 7" in any world that has a switch, a text and a number.
- Did not generalise: the families that are not acts (sources from other worlds, statistics, pictures and files) keep 163 hand patterns and had to be given a HIGHER priority than the generic forms, otherwise "chart the prices of the toys" is a `make` and "copy the chart data" a `copy`; the verb alone cannot say "delete the chart" from "clear the chart" (one form for both, the domain hook cannot see the verb). The ledger has the 10 rows by cause (reset, un-verbs, "drop", an anaphora of the open tab, the dialogue's "instead", no-chart declines).
- **[LIB]** Priority between a specific frame of a world and a generic act form needs a rule, not numbers: "a frame that names the world's own words beats the act forms" (today each extra pattern got +20 by hand). The same module should own "what is X" for a named thing of the open view, so that a bar of the open chart wins against a catalog word (`focus` from the decline hook did it for the open tab only).
- **[LIB]** A slot grammar fact found the hard way: an optional lexicon slot before a required slot does not match (`{value:L}? {noun:N}`), a lexicon word that ends in s is lemmatised away (`sideways` must also be listed as `sideway`), `words` takes at most 4 tokens unless `words(14)`; the library's form compiler should reject or fix these when the schema is generated.
- **[KNOW]** `k.actions` lacks verbs of remove ("drop") and of reset; `k.attributes` should say that a switch attribute has a verb and its un-verb (stack / unstack, show / hide), and the default of an attribute ("reset the colours"); a view that takes a set (`many`, `counts`) is still 26 hand lines because `acts.view` cannot declare it.

## LANG-EN first slice: noun phrases, coordination / ellipsis, anaphora read from English facts (worker-log "LANG-EN first slice")
- Generalised: the closed classes of English were written again in seven modules (5 lists of determiners, 5 of pronouns, 4 of prepositions, 2 of ordinals, 2 of singularisation); as facts with a view per old list, one 214-line module (`src/lang/en.mjs`) replaced them and 187 lines of code in 12 files (the algorithms moved, so the code did not shrink; the gain is one place for every word list); test-world (34 worlds, both reasoners), owner logs a / b / c, the robustness sets and the coverage grid are identical row by row, which is what "knowledge moved, behaviour did not" should look like.
- Did not generalise: the views differ by accident (`dialogue` has `many few several`, `routing` has `new`, `acts` lacks `his her its their`), so the facts could not be unified without moving numbers; the algorithms (split a coordination, shared argument, one-anaphora) are still code, only their word lists and limits are facts; negation of an act, relative clauses, "the other one" and "some of the tables" are in no module at all.
- **[LIB]** `std.grammar` should ship the facts-lookup functions first (views, inflect, fragment, agent), then the three algorithms with these cases, then a chart parser measured against the generated patterns; a `grammar.diff(viewA, viewB)` makes unifying a measured decision. The proposal is `handover/lang.en/std-grammar.md`.
- **[KNOW]** `w.lang.en` is `handover/lang.en` (schema, 11 shards: 176 argument frames, 79 phrase groups of lang.common, rules with `status=hole` for the English nothing reads); `lang.common` mixes grammar with world-specific lead-ins (`i_want_to_make_the`), which belong to the worlds that asked for them.
- Method: when a list is copied into facts, keep one named view per old copy and diff the whole gate; a differential test of the old and new function over 2,116 words found the lemmatizer equal before any suite ran.

## LANG-EN slice 2: clause cutter and argument frames as facts, then the holes one by one (worker-log "LANG-EN slice 2")
- Generalised: the clause splitter, the card-driven cutter, frame leads, correction markers, bare answers and question words now read `src/lang/en.mjs` (`handover/lang.en/shards/clauses.sop`, each old list a named view); the 238 argument frames of the acts (the old `FORMS`, `FORMS_MORE`, `FORMS_DATA`: the first extraction had 176, the 62 `acts.data` forms were missing) are facts in `frames-acts.sop` and the schema worlds generate their patterns from them, with the code arrays deleted (280 lines of form tables gone from three files). Both moves passed the strict gate: test-world (both reasoners), owner logs, rules-C robustness (1,848 rows) and the coverage grid (1,689 cells) identical row by row, generated files of the 7 schema worlds byte-identical.
- Method that made the move safe: rebuild the old structure from the facts and deep-equal it with the code arrays BEFORE deleting them (238 of 238 equal, which also exposed what the first extraction had lost: the 62 `acts.data` forms and the reader forms' `tabOnly` / `tabPriority`); then delete.
- Holes, with cases (`training/eval/lang-holes.jsonl`, 31 cases, `node scripts/eval-lang-holes.mjs`, recorded in the case corpus as source `holes`): right 10 -> 27 of 31 (new reading in `en.nounPhrase`: relative clause with "be" 0 -> 5 of 5, "some of / two of" 0 -> 4 of 4, "the other one" 2 -> 4 of 4, "or" 0 -> 3 of 3, negated request 0 -> 3 of 3 (a 4th case, a leading "never", stays open); controls 6 of 6 before and after). Not closed: temporal prepositions (2 of 5; the facts and `en.timeRelation` exist, but no world has an order of its things in time to consume them: "is Monday before Friday" is still answered as a distance), and a leading "never" (kids.tasks reads it as a statement about a task).
- What the holes showed: four of them were not missing English but WRONG ACTIONS: "make table 1 or table 3 red" changed table 3 (the last of the list), "remove table 1 or table 2" removed table 2, "make two of the tables..." added tables, "do not draw a sun" drew a sun (the form swallowed "do not" into a free capture), "remove some of the houses" removed all of them. Reading what a sentence does not say (a choice, a part of a set, a negation) is a safety property before it is a coverage one; each of these now asks or declines and changes nothing.
- Did not generalise: the NP readings plug into two resolvers (the acts executor `resolve`, art.draw `resolveNP`) because art.draw has its own noun-phrase parser (1,000 lines) and the forms' determiner words ("some", "the") are eaten by the pattern before the role capture, so the reading also has to accept "of the tables" and "other table" (the facts list those residual forms). One chart parser over the facts (`std.grammar`) would read the phrase once.
- Negation of a request is a dialogue-level decision, and the check "does a world own this negation" is the span of the reading's free captures: "Dont loop it" is the music world's loop off (a pattern that consumed the words, 399 tests expect it) and must stay; "Do not draw a sun" was read by a catch-all whose free capture held "Do not draw a". The first version intercepted both (one new test failure in media.music); the measured fix, not a list of exceptions, is the rule.
- **[LIB]** `std.grammar` should read these four facts (`relpron`, `partitive`, `other`, `choice`) and the negation lead as rules of its chart, with "the reading asked instead of guessed" as a result kind (`ask` with candidates) so every world gets it; today it is code in two resolvers.
- **[KNOW]** `w.lang.en` needs an order-in-time relation (before / after / during with the line it is measured on: days, months, seasons, the clock) in `k.time`; then `timeRelation` has a consumer and the five temporal cases close.

## Avatar switch: the avatar became the generated world (worker-log "P1 round 3", "P1 round 4")
- Generalised: one generator (`build-avatar-schema.mjs`) turns k.faces into a schema; the same generator features (canonical examples, lead and tail fillers, `tabClaim`, `accepts` decline, `outOfScope` with a clue) then served every later schema world.
- Did not generalise at first: a generated world's catch-all reading (a free text slot) captured sentences of eight other worlds on the default tab (34 test-world failures). The fix was a rule about readings, not about the avatar: a reading with free text yields to a reading with none unless the world holds the focus.
- **[LIB]** A world that declares a free-text capture needs a library-level arbitration contract ("claim" vs "decline" vs "focus"); today each host re-invents `accepts`. The library should ship the decline rule and a test that no schema world takes another world's sentence.
- **[LIB]** One undo step for several acts of one sentence (`severalActs`) and the generated-world tests (canonical examples must be readable by the world itself) are generator output, not host code.
- Catalog cost was a hidden half of the switch (steps of one turn 11,725 to 6,099 after an index by type and value in `facts.wires`): **[LIB]** `std.knowledge` readers should index a shard once and memoise a lookup within a turn.

## Diagram switch: the diagram became a schema world on a vendored engine (worker-log "diagram switch", "P1 round 3")

## Ablation: every model switched on and off, end to end (worker-log "MODEL ABLATION")
- Generalised result: on 6,100 gold turns, rules alone read 4,978 right (39 ms per message); the encoder adds about 60 right (its whole advantage is model segmentation), the T5 normalizer +28, the NX hybrid reader net -3. The three routers are within 3 messages end to end.
- Did not generalise: the NX gate and the normalizer break made-up-word probes; router A drifted (981 to 915). A model that reads 10 of 6,744 turns is not worth its latency.
- **[LIB]** The clause splitter, not the model, was what the encoder bought: the library's segmenter should be the cut point so that hosts need no encoder for clause cuts. The ablation harness (configurations x sets, cause groups from the ledger) is reusable as a library tool.
- Method: the cause key must be shared by every report (`scripts/lib/failure-cause.mjs`), otherwise group counts disagree across tools.

## Recurring lessons across landings
- A fix is accepted when it names a cause group and reports the group's count before and after; most "bugs" in the ledger are one group seen many times (boundary 1,656 of 2,504 failing cases: translation and typing, not the pipeline).
- A pair of worlds that cooperate is worth more than a feature inside one world: T1 shows 11 of 15 world pairs working with no per-pair code.
- What each landing deleted is the real measure: lines of hand-written language, general lines still in PSys2, knowledge items still in worlds. The table makes it visible; the accuracy sets only guard.

## 2026-11-14 acts bridge: domain effects through sopworld 0.29.0
- **The first switch costs more in the contract than in the code.** Moving the host's acts onto `std.schema.effect` took one 80-line module (`worlds/acts/effects.mjs`) and 12 lines in `acts.mjs`; the work was finding what the library checks before an effect (required arguments per act: placeholders), what it swallows (a throw in an effect is an `error` refusal; budget stops and bugs must be rethrown by the host) and what it needs to undo (the library calls `undo()` only inside a sentence, so the host gives one snapshot for the whole sentence).
- **All or nothing is a behaviour change worth having.** The bridge left the earlier parts of "draw a moon, a star and a ghost" done when the ghost was refused (one undo step, a wrong picture and a reply about a failure). With the library sentence nothing is kept and the reply says "I changed nothing". No test, owner log or corpus case depended on the old behaviour (0 right-to-wrong).
- **Measured, same numbers:** art.draw 564/596 ops and 561/600 matrix, diagram.sketch 756/771 both, ui.avatar 18/18 both, data.charts 258/267 ops and 267/277 matrix, plan.seating 7/7, plan.timeline 4/4: identical to before. Owner logs 45 / 10 / 14 identical, corpus 0 right-to-wrong. Cost: about 8% on a world's test run (the difference reads the `referents` signatures twice per act), a few ms per sentence of several steps (`structuredClone` of the session state).
- **Replies stay the host's.** Templates need a reply that can be made from a diff. The four domain worlds say what they did in their engines' words, so the library's `actReply` has nothing to fill; the step that unlocks it is a world whose engine returns what it did as data (a diff with the user's words), not a sentence. Until then replies are a bridge, listed in specs/21 section 10.
- **State off the wires makes the reserved-names question disappear;** it returns the day a world's things become wires, which is also the day the generic executor and its reply code can go.
## Smoke invariants after the art.draw switch (worker-log "smoke invariants after the art.draw switch")
- A red invariant is not always the invariant's subject: the network test was red because a generated catch-all reading (`show {thing:words}`) of the wrong world read a typo before the tab's repair. Read the failing assertion before blaming the named cause; the readings with and without the catalog were equal all along.
- A free-words slot in a generated pattern needs the same decline rule as any catch-all: the world's `accepts` must say what the words may be (here: names of the drawing), not accept the act.
- Two layers each kept "one step per message" (the executor's `stepGroup`, a hand merge inside `add`); the owner's rule is one step per request, so a rule stated in a spec is a test, and a spec line that contradicts a newer decision (spec 21 section on undo) is a bug to fix with the code.
- **[LIB]** The undo contract of the library should be per request with an optional "whole message" tag; hosts then do not re-implement grouping (executor, dialogue timeline and world engine each held a copy).

## Arbitration of generated worlds (worker-log "ARBITRATION")
- Generalised: four rules in the formalizer's decline step instead of per-phrase entries (tab owns its acts, full support, plain refusal, no loose tab change); arbitration known-failures 36 to 9, the 6 invariants green on the full sweep, art.draw own tests unchanged (39 failing before and after).
- Did not generalise: worlds that are hand-written catch-alls (music "another one", tic-tac-toe bare "move", kids.tasks "remove X") still claim bare sentences because they have no `accepts`; the fix belongs to their schema migration, not to the arbiter.
- **[LIB]** `accepts` needs a documented three-valued verdict (own / decline / refuse) and an optional `resolves(words)` hook; the 'focus' verdict was silently turned into `true` by the acts wrapper for as long as the hook existed, so the tab never owned its acts. A verdict that a wrapper may erase should be tested once.
- **[LIB]** A decline restored as a refusal must not run on spelling variants or one-world views of the set (it made every variant "read"); the library's arbiter should know which pass it is in.
- Method: stale fixtures hide real wins (tests used the tab id `art.draw`, which is no tab); check the fixture before the code.

## docs.editor switch to schema + acts (worker-log "docs.editor switch")
- Generalised: 360 hand patterns, a 3,067-line lexicon, 1,126 lines of descriptions, operations, templates and the matrix files (about 6,800 hand lines) became a 300-line schema, 178 lines of hooks and 28 shared frames (`acts.text`); 208 of 222 old rows pass in both reasoners, owner logs and T1 identical. The editor engine, dictation, reading and export stayed as own code.
- Did not generalise: (1) a word that is both a kind and an attribute ("text") turned the generic switch forms into "add text = text on"; (2) a free-text verb ("write", "type") is also the verb of another world's act (the drawing's save, the music's melody): claiming it while no tab is open captured "Write a melody: C D banana"; (3) a rest slot in a make form swallows a following clause ("add a list and make it numbered"); (4) indefinite and definite are the cheapest signal for make versus refer, and the forms need both.
- **[LIB]** The slot types of a form need typed lexicons (a switch form should accept only boolean attributes); the clause splitter must cut at a frame lead inside a rest slot; a domain hook needs a way to ask one question and take the next bare sentence as its answer (pending for hooks, not only the generic state); the spelling vocabulary of a world should include its lexicon words (a schema world has no literals).
- **[KNOW]** verbs of writing text ("write", "type", "jot down") are `save` in `k.actions`; a text world needs them as `make` of a text kind.
## geo.map on schema + acts: a pilot, not a switch (worker-log "geo.map pilot")
- **Generalised:** places are read by ONE path, the catalog. Five mechanisms did it with no per-world code: `needs` entries `wires:<type>` (a world asks the host for a whole collection before its synchronous operations run, `peekWires` reads it), an open class for places (`open=place`: multiword names, accent-free spellings of a vocabulary that lists accents, a place by the relation k.geo states: "the capital of Sudan"), the `needs` of the VIEW a `things.show` request names, `hooks.noState` (a world whose state has keys the executor also uses, here `focus`), and `facts` frames for questions about the attributes of a thing (what currency does X use, how big is X, is X landlocked). The gain set "place where" went from 0 of 40 (a kb of 1,001 cities) to 40 of 40 (k.geo has 1,469); the owner row "get to sudan capital" now goes to Khartoum, where the old world showed Sudan.
- **Did not generalise, and why:** the map's grammar is navigation, not editing: show / go to / zoom in / pan / "move a bit west" have no thing and no act in k.actions (`show` is a `set` verb, `move` and `pull` are `place` verbs), so about two thirds of the old patterns (216 of 329) stay as the world's own; the generated forms read the editing half (pins, highlights, lines, attribute questions) and left 55 phrasings of the dropped families unread. Half of the failing rows (112 of 195) are not language at all: the old tests encode the old table (city populations, capitals, counts), so a world that moves its facts to the catalog must also move its expected values (tests derived from the catalog, as the gain set does), or the switch rule measures the data, not the grammar.
- **Method:** (1) a vocabulary lists the words of multi-word names ("red" of the Red River, "goat" is not), so an open class by lookup needs a stricter probe than "the vocabulary has it" (colours, doc-only words, the world's own lexicons excluded) and an `accepts` that uses the same probe; (2) a generated catch-all reading (`{thing:words}`) of a world that is not the open tab claims other worlds' verbs (move, send, put) unless every act the schema declares is in the world's own act set of `accepts` (a missing act name made the map answer "Moved south" to a drawing request); (3) a priority is a decision: the clause cutter prefers "Highlight Spain" + "Portugal" when the bare place pattern of the world outranks the act form, so the generated forms of a world that also has bare patterns need `boost`; (4) the shared fact parser did not read keys with digits (`code2`): the first test of a new catalog world is its own `parseLine` on every key of its schema.
- **[LIB]** `needs` should be a declared, typed list of the catalog collections a world reads synchronously (the library's `std.knowledge` source with a `peek` of a settled collection), and the view of a world (its declaration and its `needs`) should be one object the transfer slice loads, not two hooks found by name.
- **[KNOW]** k.geo lacks region boxes and aliases, short names (Cluj, Peking), the mainland box of countries with overseas parts (France), demonyms and one consistent city population (handover/knowledge-out/catalog-lacks.md).

## 2026-11-14 k.time version 2: an order in time by lookup, typed needs (sopworld 0.39.0)
- **A relation that the catalog derives by one rule is read by one host rule.** `k.time.order` says "of two times of one cycle the smaller position is earlier"; the host reads `n`, `cycle` and `next` of the five types (`src/knowledge/time.mjs`) and compares the positions, instead of keeping a table of days or a list of "X before Y" pairs. "After" is the same fact read backwards, a month against a season is the month's `season_north` / `season_south`, an hour against a part of the day is its `part`. Nothing in PSys2 names a month any more: 33 time words of `en.local.mjs` are deleted, the catalog's names and `core.word` facts (Mon, fall, midday) replace them.
- **The language layer cuts the sentence, the knowledge layer says whether it is about time.** `en.timeComparison` returns `{ a, b, rel, hemisphere? }` for any "is A before/after/during B" and no longer checks that A and B are moments (that needed the word list that moved to the catalog); `compareTimes` returns null for a phrase that is no time, so "is the red house before the blue one" goes on to the other readers unchanged. One cut, one lookup, no list in the language layer.
- **A cycle is not a line.** "Is Friday before Monday?" is no in one week from Monday and "does spring come after winter" is yes in the way people ask it. The answer is the position in one round of the cycle, and when that is no but the successor (`next`) is the other one, the reply says yes with the round it came from ("Monday follows Sunday when the round starts again; within one round Sunday comes after Monday"). Two moments of different cycles (a day and a month) are said not to be in one cycle, not guessed. "3 o'clock" is two hours: the reply is given when 3 am and 3 pm agree and the difference is said when they do not. No hemisphere said: the north is answered and the south is mentioned in the same sentence.
- **Offline is a build step, not a fallback written by hand.** `scripts/build-bundled-subset.mjs --time-only <catalog>` writes `bundled-time.mjs` (the two shards whole, 147 wires, 17 KB), `bundled-source.mjs` serves it in the catalog's own format and `time.mjs` seeds from it; `loadTime(facts)` replaces the seed when the catalog answers. A test runs every comparison on both.
- **Order in time reaches a view through a hook, not a column.** `plan.timeline` gets `hooks.mjs` (`after`): a moment named by a time of the calendar takes its `when` from its position and keeps its cycle on the item; the row sorts a cycle at a time (months with months). A moment the user set ("set March time to 9") and a moment that is no time keep what they have. 14 lines, no word of the calendar.
- **Typed needs replace a hook that returned strings.** A world's `needs()` returned `['faces']`, `['schema', 'series']` or `wires:<type>@<world>` and `preloadThings` parsed them; now the world declares `std.schema.needs of= type= from=?` wires in its `world.sop` (the library's type, validated by the library: `validate.mjs` skips `std.*`), `needsOf(world)` reads them in the library's shape and `facts.load({ type, from })` loads each once. ## 2026-11-14 the coverage grid and the paraphrase set measured their own generators
- **A measure that is built by a generator is only as honest as the generator.** Grid v1 said 1,172 holes of 2,379 and about 565 of them (48 %) were sentences nobody says ("Put the text REL the text 1.", "Is there thing NOUN?.", "Adjust the point Zed like the red {attribute:ATTR?}.", the internal kind names `freetext` and `thing`) or sentences whose state was empty ("Find the section 1" with nothing made). - **Rule: a placeholder left in a sentence is a generator error, never a case.** `GENERATOR_ERROR` (placeholders, `V.`, `NOUN`, `REL`, `undefined`) and a check for internal kind names run on every sentence; errors are counted per world in `coverage-grid.json` (`generatorErrors`, now 0) and never reach the corpus. Forms the generator cannot fill with words the schema or the knowledge gives are counted as `skipped` with the reason, not built from guesses (the wish / valued / list / textual extension families, host acts, reader forms; 6 to 137 per world).
- **Knowledge decides what can be said.** The verb records of `k.actions` carry the attribute class a verb sets (`colour`, `size`, `orientation`) and a value of its own (`enlarge` = size larger, `hide` = visible no); so "Blow the section 1 up red" is never built (a verb with a value of its own takes no other value; a verb of an attribute class goes with a kind that has that attribute), while "Enlarge the point Zed" is built as its own cell (`k.actions:implied`) for every kind that has a size. A singleton kind (the sky, a nose) is never counted, grouped, copied or put next to another.
- **A setup per cell, and it is judged too.** Every cell makes the things it needs first (the container, the named or numbered thing, the thing it goes next to, a second thing for a group or a layout). The setup counts as confirmed when its state changed or its reply is a plain statement; a refusal or a question back ("What should I add?") leaves the cell unconfirmed and a miss there is reported apart (123 of 394 misses), because it may be the setup that is wrong (the chart world makes points from data, not from "Add a point called Zed"). ## media.music switch to schema + acts (worker-log "media.music switch")
- **Generalised:** the player's settings (tempo, volume, instrument, loop, key, pitch) are attributes and their comparatives ("faster", "louder", "higher") the shared `more` / `less` forms; "too loud / too slow / the volume is too low" is one table (a "too X" value is the opposite direction), not 60 patterns. 203 of 377 hand patterns, 46 of 100 lexicons and phrase groups and the matrix files went; 399 of 401 own rows pass in both reasoners, owner logs and T1 identical. - **Did not generalise, and why:** (1) the acts have no "run" act, so play / stop / again are modes of `pick` and the loop a switch; a thing that sounds is not a thing that is selected. (2) Notes, chords and scales are notation, not grammar: the frames capture the words and a 179-line reader reads them, and a leading "a" is a determiner or the note A depending on what follows ("a blues scale" has no root, "a minor chord" has one); the frame's determiner swallowed the note ("Play A 4" gave the thing "4"). (3) A priority is not enough to win the home tab: a generated form has `tabPriority = priority + 8`, so once the dialogue focus is on the world, kept hand patterns need an explicit `tabPriority`, and raising `priority` instead made the music read chess moves ("e2 to e4" as an interval). (4) An idiom ("turn it up", "crank it up a little", "hurry up", "full volume") is a fixed phrase for a direction of a setting: 28 hand patterns stay. (5) The discourse continues a request by replacing a TYPED slot of the last reading ("and the G major one?" replaces the note of "play the C major scale"); a reading with a free `{thing:words}` slot has nothing to replace, so the typed chord and scale readings (2 patterns) had to stay, which the music tests did not show and a dialogue test (dfix2) did. (6) Deleting patterns lowers a world's routing score (the words of its patterns): the bare "play" went to the game until the pattern got one more priority point. (7) A clause joiner ("but", "and") inside a frame still cuts the sentence; "a G chord but one note after another" is two clauses to the cutter.
