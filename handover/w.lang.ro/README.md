# handover/w.lang.ro: the Romanian grammar of PSys2 as facts, rules and a dictionary (a DRAFT for the knowledge repository's `w.lang.ro`)

Questions.md Q13 and the reviewer entries "2026-11-14 09:25 / 09:35 / 09:45 / 10:20 / 11:20". A **language world**: the only kind of world that may hold Romanian words, as data, exactly as `w.lang.en` holds English words; its keys, type names, comments and structure are English. It joins the domain worlds only through **sense ids** (k.words, k.actions, k.colours, k.geo) and the **shared frame vocabulary** of `w.lang.en` (frame names such as `acts.make.place`, role names, the relation names of `w.lang.en.prep`). No domain world gets a Romanian word. Loaded only when Romanian is the selected language.

Written in the library's `std.morph` notation (sopworld 0.52: `m.class`, `m.ending`, `m.irregular`, `m.fold`, `m.split`, `m.template`; type names renamed `w.lang.ro.*`, as the notation allows). The open lexicon is **not wires**: it is the external dictionary (TSV), forms are generated by the classes. Source of the content: the rules translator's hand-written lexicon and morphology (`src/translate`, CC0 as the rest of the project) and hand-core; no external licensed source is used yet (candidates to check before use: Universal Dependencies Romanian treebanks, Wiktionary inflection tables, open Romanian spelling lists: each licence to be read first; a share-alike source would stay in its own shard).

```
schema.sop             the types: class, ending, irregular, fold, split, template, pron, det, prep, wh, neg, numeral, lead, conj, frame, part      (95 wires)
shards/morphology.sop  17 paradigm classes with their endings by feature cell, the diacritic fold, the clitic hyphen                                (164 wires, written by scripts/build-handover-lang-ro-morph.mjs)
shards/closed.sop      determiners, pronouns (strong, clitic), prepositions as relations with the case they govern, numerals, wh words, negation, lead-ins, conjunctions (89 wires)
shards/syntax.sop      the frames in Romanian word order (same frame names as w.lang.en), the realisation templates for generation                  (57 wires)
dictionary/<l>.tsv     one row per lemma: lemma, pos:class, gender, sense id, listed forms (irregular only), English gloss; split by the folded first letter (23 files)
STATS.json             the class statistics the morphology is written against;  VALIDATION.json: the agreement of the rules with the translator's own morphology
std-grammar-ro.md      the proposal for the library functions (read a Romanian reading into act + roles, realise English or Romanian from it)
```

## The content

**Morphology as rules and classes.** Nouns: 11 plural/article classes cover 81 % (309) of the 382 dictionary nouns (`n.f.ă-e` casă/case, `n.f.ă-i` mașină/mașini, `n.f.ie-ii` rochie/rochii, `n.f.e-i` acțiune/acțiuni, `n.f.are-ări`, `n.f.are-ri`, `n.m._-i` copac/copaci, `n.m.u-i`, `n.n._-uri` lac/lacuri, `n.n._-e`, `n.n.n-ane`), each class a table of eight cells (num sg/pl x case nomacc/gendat x def no/yes): the **enclitic definite article** is the `def=yes` cell (casă -> casa, copac -> copacul, florile), the genitive-dative the `case=gendat` cell (casei, copacului, caselor). Verbs: `v.a` (cânt), `v.az` (desenez), `v.is` (-esc) with present, imperative (2sg = 3sg: desenează, mută; 2pl), subjunctive (după "să": să desenezi), participle, gerund, infinitive; 71 of the 152 verbs are listed with their own forms (irregular: a fi, a avea, a face, a da, a lua ... and every verb with a stem alternation). Adjectives: 3 classes (alb/albă/albi/albe, înalt/înaltă/înalți/înalte, frumos/frumoasă/frumoși/frumoase) + invariant; the adjective **follows** the noun and agrees. Diacritics: `fold` wires make "casa roșie" and "casa rosie" one analysis, the generator writes the diacritic spelling; `split` takes the clitic hyphen (fă-ți, arată-mi).

**Closed classes.** Determiners (un, o, niște, acest/această, acel/acea, possessives) with `same_as` the `w.lang.en.det` they mean; pronouns strong and clitic (mi, ți, îl, o, îi, le) with case and `refers`; **prepositions as relations**: lângă = `near`, deasupra = `above`, sub = `below`, pe = `on`, în = `in`, în spatele = `behind`, în fața = `front`, între = `between`, după / înainte de = `after` / `before`, la stânga / la dreapta = `left` / `right`: the `rel` and `space` of `w.lang.en.prep` (so they join `k.layouts` and `k.time` by the same names) and `govern` = the case of the noun phrase (acc: lângă casă; gen: deasupra casei, în spatele casei); numerals with gender (doi / două), "de" from 20; wh words (ce, care, cine, unde, când, de ce, cum, câte / câți, cât) with `asks` as in `w.lang.en.wh`; negation (nu before the verb, fără before a noun, niciodată); lead-ins by kind (polite, desire, ability, filler, greeting); conjunctions.

**Word order, negation, questions** are in the header of `shards/syntax.sop` and as frames: requests are verb-first imperatives (or a subjunctive after a lead-in), the adjective follows the noun, a bare singular count noun after a preposition reads as definite, "nu" stands before the verb, a question keeps statement order with the wh word first, "are" means has (Ana are 8 creioane), a colour complement of "fă" is the object's adjective (fă casa roșie = make the house red); the dative clitic "-ți" on an avatar request is "your" in English (fă-ți ochii verzi = make your eyes green). 11 frames (`ro.make`, `ro.make.place`, `ro.change.value`, `ro.place`, `ro.remove`, `ro.ask.what.is`, `ro.ask.where`, `ro.ask.count.attr`, `ro.ask.count.at`, `ro.ask.capital`, `ro.ask.calc`) point at the `w.lang.en` frame of the same act, and 9 realisation templates say the same acts in Romanian (for replies and for generation tests).

## Mapping Romanian lemmas to the domain worlds (only by sense id)

| Romanian word class | joins | example (dictionary row) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| noun | `k.words.<word>.noun.1` | `casă -> n.f.ă-e, f, k.words.house.noun.1` |
| verb | `k.actions.verb.<act>.<verb>` (the act and the verb text of k.actions) | `muta -> v.a, k.actions.verb.place.move`; `șterge -> k.actions.verb.remove.delete`; `desena -> k.actions.verb.make.draw`; `adăuga -> k.actions.verb.make.add`; `pune -> k.actions.verb.place.put` |
| colour adjective | `k.colours.psys2.<colour>` | `roșu -> a._-ă-i-e, k.colours.psys2.red` |
| other adjective | `k.words.<word>.adj.1` | `mare -> k.words.big.adj.1` |
| place name | `k.geo` by name (nominative, genitive pairs) | `Franța, Franței -> France` |
| preposition | the `rel` of `w.lang.en.prep` | `lângă -> near` |
| frame | the `w.lang.en` frame of the same act | `ro.make.place -> acts.make.place` |

The dictionary has 632 lemmas (382 nouns, 152 verbs, 98 adjectives): exactly the lemmas that the translator analyses in the app's Romanian sets; 523 of them already carry a catalog sense id, the rest need a `k.words` sense or have none yet (the English gloss is in the last column; "Missing senses" = the asks to the knowledge repository).

A session loads the four wire files (56.5 KB raw, 10.2 KB gzip) once and, per sentence, the dictionary files of the first letters of its lemmas: mean 10.3 KB raw (3.8 KB gzip), max 28.3 KB over the 5,958 hand-written sentences of the meaning set. Splitting by first letter is too coarse: two letters (or a per-word index) would bring the mean under 2 KB; the ask is the library's dictionary adapter reading a sorted file by key range. The wires are larger than needed (the `core.source` field repeats on every line): the library's compact notation should drop it per shard.

## How good the rules are (before any hand list)

`VALIDATION.json`: for every dictionary lemma of a paradigm class the forms the rules generate must be forms the translator's own analyser accepts for that lemma. **2,466 of 2,500 forms (98.6 %), 384 of 410 lemmas with every form right.** The 34 disagreements are mostly the TRANSLATOR's gaps, not the rules': it does not accept the correct genitives capturii, găinii, culorii, florii, and some -ia verbs (copiază). The class table was first wrong in three places that the check found (-ie nouns need `a`, not `ea`; -esc verbs; adjectives with t > ț and s > ș), and was fixed. Coverage of words: the translator's analyser covers 38,384 of 47,994 word tokens of the Romanian sets (80.0 %), which is the number the dictionary must beat; the rest are names, English words in Romanian text, typos and words the lexicon lacks.

## What the grammar path measured (prototype `src/translate/grammar-ro.mjs`, a flag, not the default)

The prototype reads the most frequent request shapes (draw / make with colour, number and place; change colour; move; remove; show; wear) and the question shapes (capital, where, how much, how many parts, what sound, what day after, what is, colour mix) and the word-problem sentences (has / gets / eats / gives, how many left / altogether) into a FRAME (act + roles) and realises English from it; it **abstains** outside those shapes. Measured on `training/eval/ro-en-meaning` (README there): it answers 19.7 % of the rows and when it answers the reading equals the reference reading **85.9 %** of the time (rules 79.7, M4 with the model 74.0, raw model 58.8 on the same rows); in a chain "grammar first, then M4 with the model" the whole set goes from 55.7 % to 58.0 % kept (hand-written non-template, non-operational rows: canon +0.3, real +2.9, talk +3.7 points). The rules translator is as precise as the grammar on the shapes both cover (81.8 against 83.1 on hand-written non-template rows): the grammar's gain is on shapes the rules got wrong (-ți possessives, "ce spune", story give), its limit is coverage (the open lexicon: "cort", "acoperiș" are not in the lexicon, so "Ce este un cort?" abstains).

## Asks, open points

- **[LIB]** the `alt` per dictionary row and several alternations per cell (carte/cărți, masă/mese, floare/flori), agreement by unification across a chunk, `abstain` with a trace and a `coverage` call, a dictionary adapter reading a sorted TSV by key range (see std-grammar-ro.md). Feature names used: num sg/pl, case nomacc/gendat/voc, def yes/no, gender m/f/n, person 1/2/3, tense pres/impf/perf/pqp/subj/imper/inf/ger/part (the reviewer's suggestion), plus `mood` on frame parts.
- **Senses**: 109 of the 632 lemmas have no catalog sense id yet (listed by `awk -F'\t' '$4==""' dictionary/*.tsv`).
- **Not written yet**: the imperfect, perfect and pluperfect (replies and statements need them: "am desenat"), the vocative, the reflexive verb forms beyond the clitic hyphen, the noun classes with fewer than 4 lemmas (listed as `irregular` rows), numerals above 10 as wires (the library's number reader handles them), partitives ("unele dintre"), "celălalt / cealaltă", clitic doubling.
- **Do not publish before** the reviewer reads the table in training/eval/ro-en-meaning/README.md and decides the default per language: the numbers say the grammar path is a precise first reader for the narrow shapes, not a replacement of the translators.
