# 16. World-aware reading (FORMALIZE-FROM-WORLDS, E2)

Status: **on by default as `veto`** (user decision 2026-11-12; `worldAware: 'off'` or `PSYS2_WORLD_AWARE=off` turns it off; the constant is `DEFAULT_WORLD_AWARE` in `world-aware.mjs`). Code: `src/dialog/world-aware.mjs`, `ReasonerSession.score` / `peek` in `src/reasoner/index.mjs`, the `pick` hook in `src/formalizer/rules/index.mjs`, the `checkReferents` hooks of `worlds/art.draw/ops.mjs` and `worlds/diagram.sketch/ops.mjs`. Measurement: `scripts/e2-world-aware.mjs`, results in `training/results/e2-world-aware-2026-11-12.{json,md}`.

## 1. What it is for

The formalizer reads a message into wires without looking at the state of any world. The error audit (E1) says that most English errors are of the kind the state or the schema could settle (classes c, d, e, h), and that a wrong answer is worse than "not understood". This layer looks at the reading the pipeline is about to act on, and at the other readings it found, and judges them against the world instances **without telling anything** (the library's `world.score`, which runs each candidate in a transaction that is undone). It never invents a reading. It can only (a) refuse to act on a reading, saying why, and (b) prefer one of the readings the formalizers already found.

## 2. The option

`worldAware` is a session option of `engine.createSession` (`'off' | 'veto' | 'choose' | 'observe' | 'veto-strict' | 'choose-strict'`, default `'veto'`; a per-message option of `send` overrides it). The environment variable `PSYS2_WORLD_AWARE` sets the default of command-line tools; `scripts/e1-collect.mjs --world-aware <mode>` passes it to the evaluation. With `off` nothing in this spec runs: the turn is byte for byte the shipped one.

| mode | what it does |
| --- | --- |
| `veto` | A reading that fails one of the checks of section 3 is not acted on. The clause becomes "not understood", or a question or explanation built from the world. |
| `choose` | `veto`, and where the rules (or the best-effort reader) found several readings of one clause, they are scored and put in the order of section 4. |
| `observe` | Diagnostic: judges and records on the segment (`wa`, `pick`), changes nothing. |
| `veto-strict`, `choose-strict` | The same, and a reading that leaves out content words through the partial-understanding window (`words-partial`) is vetoed too. Measured to lose more right answers than it prevents wrong ones (section 6), so it is not part of `veto`. |

## 3. The veto (a reading is dropped for a named reason)

Each reason is recorded on the replaced segment as `veto: { kind, why, say?, offer? }` and in the trace.

1. **`no-referent`**: the reading names something the world does not have. The world says so through an optional read-only hook `checkReferents(ctx, { action, args })` in its `ops.mjs` (the same kind of hook as `referents`, `summary`: spec 11). It returns `{ missing: [{ name, kind }], here, where, offer? }`. The host composes the reply, nothing else:
   - with `offer`: "I can't find a box called “End”. Shall I add it?" (the user's label in quotes, the user's noun, else the world's everyday word, never "shape") and a pending offer whose "yes" runs `offer` (the diagram: `add a rectangle called End and connect Start to End`);
   - without: "I can't find “dragon” in the picture. In the picture I can see: a yellow house." (or "The picture is empty.").
   Today `art.draw` (`draw.remove`, and the noun phrases `t` and `o` of the others) and `diagram.sketch` (`diagram.connect`) have the hook. A world that has none is not checked. The hook may not change state: the host removes the empty state key that the hook's context creates for a world that has none.
2. **`no-game`**: removed 2026-11-15 (architecture review; no world has had a game since the tab cut, ADR-010: the test could not fire). A games approach that comes back brings its own veto with its own measurement.
3. **`words-best-effort`**: the best-effort reader (spec 03 section 7.15) executes a guess of loose words and says "I did not use ...". With the option on, a first candidate that leaves out content words is not executed: the reply is "Did you mean ...?" with the candidates (`suggestions`). A word is **content** unless it is a filler (`FILLER` in the module: please, now, just, hesitations and chat abbreviations), a word of two letters or fewer, a Romanian word of three letters or fewer (a function word the translator left), or a word that the reading spells another way (edit distance 1, or 2 from six letters: "lie" for "like", "abort" for "about", a corrected typo is not a word left out).
4. **`words-partial`** (`-strict` modes only): the same test for the readings of the partial-understanding window (spec 03 section 7.10).

**Nothing is half done.** When a clause of a message is vetoed for `no-referent`, `words-partial` or `words-best-effort`, the readable clauses of the same message are held back too (`veto.kind = held`, "I did not act on ... because I could not read all of your message. Please say it again."): a later clause may rest on an earlier one ("Azi este vineri. Mâine ce zi este?" must not tell the first and lose the second). Clauses the formalizer already left unparsed keep the shipped partial execution. A `no-game` veto does not hold the others: it is usually a spurious extra reading of a correction ("No, the editor", "sorry, I meant o"), and the other clauses of that message are the real ones.

What the library's `score` adds to the veto: for a reading that is a request or question of a matrix world whose action or question the matrix declares, `score` runs it on the instance (`ok`, `violations`, `lacks`, `action.available`, `answer`). A refusal by the world's rules ("There is nothing to undo", "that does not add up") is already the right answer with a reason, so it is **recorded** (`wa`) and **not vetoed**: vetoing it would turn a correct explanation into "not understood" (measured: 98 right against 7 wrong among the refused readings). Most actions are still the operations' (spec 15: the matrix declares few of them), so `score` cannot judge them (`wa.judged = false`); that is why `no-referent` and `no-game` read the worlds' own state through hooks and the session context.

## 4. The choice (the library's order, one host criterion)

For one clause, the candidate readings are: the distinct rules readings (`pick` hook of `formalize`, at most four, in the router's order), and the verified candidates of the best-effort reader. Each is scored with `ReasonerSession.score` (the library's `score` on the instance of the world that owns its request or question, or on a fork of the base when the session has none: the same state, `peek`), and ordered with the library's `rank` and its default `RANKING`: **consistent, referents left ambiguous, lacks, words left out, assumptions**, then the order given (the router's). Every place has an explanation (`why`, `on`, `lostTo`), recorded in `seg.pick`.

On top of the library's order, one host criterion (item 2; item 1 is the library's now) (`pick.libraryDefault` is the library's own first choice):
1. **A question whose answer is not computed yet lacks nothing** is the library's own since sopworld 0.13.1 (J1: `score` understands a question given as a wire, and a computed answer not made yet is not a lack). The host's zeroing of the `lacks` of a question reading, which sopworld 0.12 made necessary, is gone; `libraryOrder` and the host order are the same list. Gate: `training/results/e2-j1-2026-11-12.md`.
2. (removed 2026-11-15 with `no-game`: a move in a game that is not running came last.)

An action the matrix does not declare is scored too (sopworld 0.12): the library judges it by a fallback (referents, slots, denies) and says so in `judged` / `judgedBy`; `ReasonerSession.score` passes both through and adds `libVeto`, the reasons of the library's `veto(result)` for the reading. `libVeto` is recorded in `wa` (trace only). It was an opt-in veto kind (`library`) behind `PSYS2_WA_KINDS` (measured in `training/results/e2-world-aware-2026-11-12b.md`, never a default); the kind and the switch were deleted in P6 (section 15).

`choose` reorders only when the library's first reading differs from the router's (a tie keeps the router's order). Not implemented, and why: the dispatcher's top-k (the encoder strategy returns one reading per clause), the library's split candidates for compound messages as an acting reading (since READER-ROUTE: section 7, behind its own option), the dialogue layer's follow-up readings (the discourse layer changes its state before it reads, so a second reading would have to be undone), the Romanian translator route (one candidate).

## 5. Rules of the layer

- Read-only: `score` and `peek` do not change the instance (`version`, wires and log are unchanged; `tests/world-aware.test.mjs`); a hook changes nothing in the session state.
- Bounded: every loop of the layer ticks the turn budget (`world-aware.segment`, `world-aware.pick`); at most four readings are scored per clause; the library's own `score` takes the turn's budget.
- Never throws: a failure to judge keeps the reading.
- Quoting: the clarifying replies quote the user's words ("There is no dragon in the picture"), as the undo replies do.
- English only inside the layer; a Romanian message is judged on its English reading, and its leftover words (the translator's) of three letters or fewer are not content.

## 7. The reader as a third source of readings (READER-ROUTE)

Code: `src/dialog/reader-route.mjs`, the stage `_withReader` of `src/dialog/index.mjs`, the loader flag in `src/worlds/index.mjs`. Measurement: `scripts/e1-collect.mjs --reader <mode>`, `scripts/reader-route-report.mjs`, `scripts/e2-splits.mjs`; results in `training/results/reader-route-2026-11-12.{json,md}`. Tests: `tests/reader-route.test.mjs`.

### 7.1 What it is

The library's reader core (`interpret` over the English `core.word` facts of a world's `describe.sop`, split candidates, `score`, `rank`) reads a clause and gives readings. They are turned into the **same dialogue wires the rules produce**: the description's own `emit` template filled with what the library found, normalized and validated like the rules' wires (`buildWires`, `normalizeWire`, `validateWire`). The segment is marked `strategy: 'reader'` and carries `reader` (the world, the operation, what was assumed, why it was taken). It is a third source next to the rules and the encoder and uses the same judging (`ReasonerSession.score`).

### 7.2 The option

`reader` is a session option of `engine.createSession` (`'off' | 'fill' | 'choose'`, default `'off'`; a per-message option of `send` overrides it; the environment variable `PSYS2_READER` sets the default of command-line tools; `scripts/e1-collect.mjs --reader <mode>` passes it to the evaluation). `describe.sop` is read in full by the loader only when the flag is on (`PSYS2_READER` at engine creation; otherwise only its `slot=` declarations). With `off` nothing in this section runs and the turn is byte for byte the shipped one.

| mode | what it does |
| --- | --- |
| `fill` | A clause that nothing read (unparsed, after the rules, the encoder, the leniency stages and the best-effort reader) takes the reader's reading when the reader has one that passes section 7.4. |
| `choose` | `fill`, and a clause that was read takes the reader's reading when the library's `rank` (`ReasonerSession.score` over the current reading with the words it leaves out, and the reader's) puts the reader's first. A tie keeps the current reading (the given order). Only a rival reading of the world the clause was read in, or of the open tab, is considered. |

The reader reads only the worlds the user is in (the open tab's world, the modal world, the running game's world). A world with no English description is not read. After the stage, the world-aware layer (sections 2 to 5) judges the reader's reading like any other (`veto`, `choose`).

### 7.3 The mapping of `means`

The 0.12 finding: with the words told to the world's matrix instance, only kids.shop's words resolved (geo.map 341 and docs.editor 247 `unknown-word` warnings), because a `means` of a description word is an **emit key** (`geo.capital`, `doc.addHeading`), not a name the matrix declares (the matrices declare few actions, spec 15). The reader therefore keeps a small **reader world** (a library `World`, built once per loaded world set, memoized, never holding user state) in which every `means` has a place the library knows:

- every description (`core.describe`) is a **type** named by its id (not by its `op`: several descriptions may share one `op` and differ in emit and words, as a title and a heading do);
- every argument is a **slot** of that type (`op.arg` is the key the words of an argument mean; a quantity slot `qty=` is a number slot); a `number` argument is a number slot, an argument of a lexicon class (`lex.x`) is a slot that holds a wire of the class, whose wires are the lexicon's words (identifying key `name`), any other class (`rest`, `noun`, `words`) is a free string slot;
- the words of a description are told per description and role (`verb`, `tverb`, `noun`, `ask`, `rest`, `pre`, `mid`, `subject` mean the type; `prep.x`, `det.x`, `unit.x` mean the key `type.x`); a text of several words is one token with `_` (the library keeps the quotes of a quoted text), and the host merges the tokens of a clause that spell such a word or a name ("how many people live in", "new_york") before the library sees them; a plural name is read as its singular (the rules' lemma).

So a `means` that names an operation, an argument or a value always resolves (`readerCoverage` reports it per world; the tests require no `unknown-word` warning and no unused word). Where the matrix does declare the name (kids.shop) the reading is the same; the matrix is not touched: the reading is judged on the real instance afterwards, as the rules' readings are.

### 7.4 What the reader may take (the policy, `READER_POLICY`)

A reading is taken only when all of these hold; each was added because a measurement showed a wrong answer without it.

1. It leaves nothing out: every word of the clause is a word of the chosen description (role words, argument words), the value of an argument, a glue word (the host's fillers, question words, auxiliaries, prepositions), or the spelling the library corrected (`assumes`). A split candidate must read every frame of the split: since sopworld 0.13.2 (R14.1) a frame the library cannot read stays as an unresolved part (`unread: true`, its words in `unresolved`), and a split with such a part is no reading.
2. A word the library read as a near known word is corrected by one letter at most (`maxAssume`; "moved" read as "money" is not a spelling of it).
3. It rests on a content word of the description (`anchor`): one of its role words that is not glue, spelled as in the description or one letter away. "Is Kyoto" alone is not "is Kyoto landlocked".
4. It is unambiguous: no reading of another set of wires is as good on every criterion (words left out, assumptions, a verb word rather than an implied verb, words used) in a world as near the conversation. Two descriptions that emit the same wires are one reading.
5. Every number of the clause is a value of the reading.
6. No negation in the clause (the reader has no way to say it).
7. **No free text** (`freeText: false`): an argument that takes any words (class `rest`, `noun`, `words`) is not filled by the reader. The descriptions cover only part of a world, so a word they do not know may be content that the rules know ("make the heading bigger", "heading underline", "center the title" were read as headings with that text in the first measurement: 30 wrong answers). Descriptions with such an argument give the reader no reading.

### 7.5 Rules of the stage

- Read-only: `interpret`, `score` and `rank` run on the reader world and on forks; nothing is told to any world (`tests/reader-route.test.mjs`).
- Bounded: the stage ticks the turn budget (`dialog.reader`, `reader.world`, `reader.tokens`, `reader.build`); `interpret` takes the library's own limit (12 candidates a clause).
- Never throws: a failure keeps the reading.
- English only: a Romanian message is read through the translator first and then the same English route.
- The follow-up readings of the library (`previous`, `correction`, `other`) are not used: the discourse layer has already handled follow-ups before the formalizer runs.

## 8. One declaration of what an argument fills (Q2 item 2, J2; sopworld 0.13.1)

A host-run action (one the operations carry out and the matrix does not declare) is declared once, in SOP Lang and English, by `core.describe.arg` with a `slot=<type>.<key>` that names the slot of the world's legend the argument fills:

```
@describe.chess.level.set.n core.describe.arg
    of=$describe.chess.level.set
    name=n
    class=number
    slot=chess.game.level
```

- **Where.** In the world's `describe.sop`, on the `core.describe.arg` wire of the argument (a world that has wording there, like `games.chess`, adds one line to the wire it already has). `slotDeclarations(text)` (`src/worlds/describe.mjs`) cuts a `describe.sop` down to the `core.describe.arg` wires that have a `slot=` and the `core.describe` wires they belong to; the loader reads only that cut when the generator and the reader are off, and `scripts/build-webchat.mjs` ships only that cut (never the wording). A world's `argSlots` is `action -> (argument -> slot)`. A description without wording generates nothing, so a file that only declares slots is allowed.
- **The library's judgement (J2).** When the reasoner builds an instance it derives, for every declared action the matrix does not declare, a type named like the action whose slots are the arguments, with the kind, enum and range of the slot they name (`createReasoner`, "parameter types"); a slot the legends do not declare is a load problem. The library's `score` then judges a reading of that action by its fallback (a value that does not fit its slot's kind, enum or range makes it inconsistent: `judgedBy` `slots`; all that could run passing: `fallback`). Nothing else changes: no deny of the world is touched, no required parameter is declared (so none lacks).
- **A declaration is only right when the argument is canonical.** `ui.rate value` was tried against `ui.shell.view.rate` (integer, 50 to 200): the formalizer emits both "150" and "1.5" for a rate, so "Set the speech rate to 1.5" was judged out of range and `choose` made 2 new wrong answers; the declaration was removed. Likewise the enums of `art.draw` (`draw.add kind`, `draw.mood w`, `draw.ground g`) are canonical names while the arguments are the user's words ("puppy", "evening"): not declared. A canonical value needs the formalizer to emit it (an `emit` that says the canonical word), not the library to guess.
- **What is not derived (and why).** `checkReferents` (section 3) stays a hook of `art.draw` and `diagram.sketch`. Its arguments are noun phrases and a whole segment (`diagram.connect arg.text="connect A to B with a line"`, `draw.remove arg.phrase="the red house"`), which no argument-to-slot declaration can resolve: the names are inside the text, and finding them is the world's reading. It needs the patterns to emit the names as arguments (`arg.from`, `arg.to`), which changes the patterns of the diagram and the drawing and is a separate task. A hook for a referent that is a plain argument (a value outside the enum of its slot is a thing the world does not have) is what the declaration would give; no world has such an argument yet.
- **Measurement** (`training/results/e2-arg-slots-2026-11-12.md`): with `chess.level n` declared, 28 more readings are judged (`fallback`), none changes outcome, 0 new wrong in `veto` (default) and `choose`.



## 9. The typed reading step (abstraction round item 1; sopworld 0.72 `typedLexicon`)

**Status (P6, 2026-11-15): off by default, `PSYS2_TYPED=on` turns it on (a named diagnostic switch).** See section 15 for the measurement.

Code: `src/dialog/typed-reader.mjs`, the stage `_withTypedReader` of `src/dialog/index.mjs` (after the rules, the best-effort and the reader route, before the knowledge lookup and the world-aware review). Tests: `tests/typed-reader.test.mjs`.

**What it does.** A clause the rules read completely and the world REFUSED is read again by the types of its words. "Change the unit of timeline 1 to day." is read by the frame `acts.change.of` with the thing "timeline" and the value "1 to day" (a free-text role stops at the number): the world answers "1 to day is not a unit I know". The step types the world from its own facts (each thing of the session as a wire with its label "timeline 1", its kind and attributes; each value an attribute can take, from the schema or, for a colour, from the colours the readers take), asks the library's `typedLexicon` for the type of every word, and reads the free-text roles of the refused reading again from the words they took together: the role of the type thing takes the words that name a thing of the session, the role of the type value takes the word typed as a value of the attribute the reading holds, and what is left must be function words (to, of, the). Word order only splits the words; the types assign them.

**It proposes; the world decides; all or nothing.**
1. It runs only when the world refuses (a run on a fork of the session, `_outcomes`: an answer with a `fallback` code and no change). A reading the world accepts, an unparsed clause and a clause nobody read are never touched.
2. Every word of the region must be placed, both roles filled, the wires rebuilt with the world's own pattern (`buildWires`) must validate, and the world must then act on the new reading on the fork (supported, not refused, something changed). Otherwise the original reading stays, whole.
3. No sentence is generated, nothing is told to any world, and no pattern, word list or lexicon entry is added: the same step reads every schema world (plan.timeline, plan.seating, art.draw ...). The segment carries `typed: { from, to, id }`.

**Library limit.** `readByTypes` returns `ask`, `find` and `act` frames whose act arguments are things; it has no frame for "set an attribute to a value", so this step uses the library's typing (`typedLexicon`) and assembles the arguments by type itself. Unparsed clauses (the paraphrases of the learning curve: "cow sound?") need the frames of `readByTypes` mapped to the world's wires: not done.

## Related cases of a case (measurement, `scripts/related-cases.mjs`, 2026-11-14; called related cases until sopworld 0.111)

A failure is a class, and a fix is judged on its related cases (owner, 2026-11-14; metamorphic testing, see the glossary). The library gives the cases: `relatedCases(world, { text, frame }, { distance, axes, read, known, n, seed })` lifts a case up the kinds (a filler to its kind and the kind's parent) and along the keys (the other aspects of the same holder), comes down to other members and keeps the valid ones (an answer exists, the new wording reads back to the same frame, not a known case). Distance 1 = siblings, 2 = first related cases, 3 = further. The frame comes from `readByTypes(world, text)`. The host adds only what the library cannot know: the pipeline, the judge and the table. **There is no hand-written list of related cases anywhere, not even a temporary one.**

- **Library.** The vendored sopworld when it exports `relatedCases` and `readByTypes`, else `--lib <sopworld checkout>` (its `docs/sopworld.mjs` bundle or `src/index.mjs`). The checkout is measurement only and is never imported by `src/`. A library without `relatedCases` is an error, not a fallback.
- **The typed world.** `relatedCases` works on a library `World` of typed relations (things with `name`, `kind`/`class`, keys). The runtime worlds' matrix instances do not hold that knowledge (the animals are in the catalog, not in the instance), so the host builds the World from the knowledge catalog the Talk tab is given: `--catalog` (default `tests/fixtures/catalog`), `--facts k.animals,...|all` (a text the library refuses is left out and listed).
- **Run.** Every valid generated related case goes to a fresh session with the tab open (`--tab`, default `talk`), once per reasoner (`--reasoner ops|matrix|both`, a child process each, because the reasoner is fixed at engine creation).
- **Judge** (`judge` in `scripts/lib/related-cases-host.mjs`): by state and reply. Right when every answer value the library computed from the world is said in the reply (a holder id of a `find` is read as its name, a small number may be its word) and a question changed no world (the Talk record, `session.things` and the shell are the view of the question and are not compared). Wrong otherwise; `no_reply` for an empty reply.
- **Table.** Per distance x axis, the five dimensions of the owner: filler kind, aspect, act family, alternation, world structure. The library (0.73 through 0.75) generates the first two only (its `axes` option; the 0.75 alternations and nearWords are separate functions the adapter does not call yet) (`axes`: `filler`, `key`, `wording`); the other three are printed as "not generated". A case the types do not read has no frame; a case whose frame has no holder (a `find`, an `act`) has no related cases: both are listed per case, so "no related cases" is visible and never counted as a pass.
- **Batches.** `--batch build=<b>` or `class=<prefix>` takes the texts of `training/eval/failures.jsonl`; `file=<jsonl>` takes `{ text, tab }` rows. Results go to `training/results/related-cases/<date>-<label>.json` (runs before the rename stay in `training/results/related cases/`).

- **Limits seen on 0.73.** The `key` dimension lists every key of the holder, including metadata (`drawing`, `named`, `cannot`), so some "aspects" have no natural wording ("what cannot does a cow make"); a multi-word holder name ("Tasmanian devil") is a related case the spell checker reads as another word. `readByTypes` reads many action sentences as `find` frames of one word; those have no holder and no related cases. Tests: `tests/related cases-host.test.mjs` (a fake library with the interface above).


## 10. Reading steps for unparsed clauses (abstraction round items 2 and 3; sopworld 0.74 `nearWords`, 0.75 `normalise`)

**Status (P6, 2026-11-15): every step of this section is off by default; `PSYS2_ALTERNATIONS=on`, `PSYS2_NEAR=on`, `PSYS2_SLOTS=on` turn one on (named diagnostic switches; the `=off` forms below are the old defaults).** With no voice switched on, `withVariants` is not called. See section 15.

Code: `src/dialog/variants.mjs`, the stage `withVariants` of `src/dialog/index.mjs` (after the reader route, before the typed step of section 9). Tests: `tests/variants.test.mjs`.

A clause nobody read (`unparsed`) is read again by three steps in this order, each behind a switch for A/B runs. A step proposes another reading; the world decides (a run on a fork must support the new reading and must not end in the world's own refusal; a statement is accepted on its validated wires); all or nothing (a clause with two different acceptable readings keeps none; the original stays whole when no step succeeds); nothing is told to a world, no sentence is generated, no pattern or lexicon entry is added.
1. **Alternations** (`PSYS2_ALTERNATIONS=off`): `normalise` over the `w.lang.en.alternation` facts of the catalog (passive, wrappers such as "could you", question and command, dative shift) brings the clause to its canonical form and the rules read that. The rules are knowledge: the catalog published none at the time of writing, so the step is a no-op until it does (it reads `facts.wires('w.lang.en.alternation')`).
2. **Near words** (`PSYS2_NEAR=off`): a word that none of the worlds the clause could belong to knows (the open tab first, then the recent ones and the suggested ones) is looked up in the word senses of the catalog (`k.words.sense`, by its lemma candidates) and read as its nearest word when the library's `nearWords` costs 3 or less (synonym 1, kind 2, further kinds 3) and the world knows that word. At most two unknown words; the readings are compared by their wires.
3. **Slot types** (`PSYS2_SLOTS=off`): the content words of the clause are assigned, whatever their order, to the typed slots of one pattern of a world (a slot of a lexicon class takes the word of that lexicon); the pattern's own words and the function words are the rest; no content word may be left over and the pattern's own words must anchor the reading; the assignment in a pattern must be unique and the world the person is in decides first. It is the frame of `readByTypes` (`ask`, `find`) assembled from the lexicons of the world: the typed fact world that `readByTypes` needs exists for the schema worlds (section 9) but not for the matrix worlds. The talk frames take free words (`{a:words(1)}`), which this step does not read.

The segment carries `variant: { via, from, to, ... }`.

## 12. Complete readings: a reading that leaves content words unread is not final (Q15 items 2 and 3; sopworld 0.104 completeness)

Code: `src/formalizer/completeness.mjs` (`annotateUnread`, `isIncomplete`, the switch), the matcher's `skipped` ranges (`src/formalizer/rules/matcher.mjs`), `runOnSegments` and the incomplete-reading eligibility in `src/dialog/variants.mjs`, the stage in `src/dialog/index.mjs` (`_sendCore`), arbitration in `src/formalizer/routing.mjs` (`setWorldChooser`). Tests: `tests/completeness.test.mjs`.

**What is unread.** A reading is the best match of a clause. Every candidate match of the clause carries `unread`, the content words (not function words, not fillers) that nothing reads: (1) the words a `...` skip of its pattern passed over; (2) a question word (what, how, which, who, where, when, why) inside a free-text capture (`words`): the capture swallowed another clause ("what sound does a cow make what 7 plus 5" read as one `change` of the music world); (3) a word inside a free-text capture of this reading that a reading of ANOTHER world reads with a literal or a typed slot of its own ("move the house upsides" in the music world while the drawing world has the house). `rest` captures are dictation, free by design, and never count. No word list: (2) is the structure of the clause, (3) is competition between the readings of the same sentence. `segment.match.unread` carries it. This is the host's version of the library's `readByTypes(...).left` / `complete`: the matrix worlds have no typed fact world at runtime (sections 9 to 11), so the library's own measure cannot be used on them.

**Consequences (switch `PSYS2_COMPLETE=off|hold|ask`).**
1. *Other readers first.* An incomplete reading is eligible for the same steps as an unread clause (`withVariants`: paraphrase rewrite rules, canonicalise, alternations, near words, slot types) plus the run-on step: the library's `splitRunOn` cuts the clause where its parts read best (each part read by the rules, a part scores its words read minus 3 per word unread), every part must then be a complete reading and the world must accept all of them. A variant is accepted only if it is itself complete (no unread words), the world decides, all or nothing.
2. *Nothing destructive on a half-read sentence.* An incomplete clause is "open" like an unread clause: a destructive clause next to it is held, and an incomplete destructive clause is never executed ("I understood only part of ...: I did not use ..., so I did not do it. What did you mean?").
3. *Then a clarifying ask* (`ask` only): any incomplete reading that no voice completed is asked, not executed. `hold` keeps the reading for non-destructive acts.
4. *Arbitration by coverage* (`hold` and `ask`): when several worlds read the sentence and some reading has unread words, the worlds' best readings are ordered by the library's decision for kind `world` (std.decide, the criteria of `arbitrate`): coverage (fewer unread words), then the open tab or the stronger tier (named world, navigation, recent worlds), then priority; the tiers of `routing.mjs` feed the last two criteria, so a sentence with no unread word is routed exactly as before. The decision records `arbitration: { by: 'coverage', moved, ranked }`.

**Ensemble.** Superseded by section 13: the voices run side by side and `readEnsemble` decides on the typed world of the clause's world (`PSYS2_ENSEMBLE=on`; off by default since P6, section 15). The run-on step is `PSYS2_RUNON=on` (off by default since P6).

**First result (2026-11-15, untyped heuristic; superseded by section 13, where the default became `hold`).** The curve does not move (paraphrases-rt 14 right / 53 wrong / 16 not understood / 2 asked in all three modes, no row changes in the held-out third; related cases per clause identical; schema-alone 59 of 60 replies identical; dice identical) and the invariants break: `test-world all` fails 61 rows with `off`, 83 with `hold`, 101 with `ask` (a free-text `what` in the drawing world's colour questions and the docs reader's counts are legitimate readings that the host's unread test cannot tell from a swallowed clause; `ask` turns them into questions). The code stays behind `PSYS2_COMPLETE=hold|ask`; the next step needs the typed world of the matrix worlds (the library's own `left` / `complete`), not a host heuristic.
**Still not wired.** `continueAct` (0.104; verbless continuations) and `patternReader` (mined patterns of a typed world): the typed worlds of section 13 now exist, wiring them is a later step; the host's own `discourse.ellipsis` keeps serving the continuations.

## 13. Typed worlds: completeness, arbitration and the ensemble read by types (Q16; sopworld 0.114 `typedWorldFor`, `fitsSlot`)

Code: `scripts/build-typed-worlds.mjs` (build time), `worlds/<id>/typed.json` (generated, never edited; `worlds/<id>/typed.sop` hand-written joins), `src/formalizer/typed-world.mjs` (`createTypedWorlds`, `targetsOf`, `unreadBy`, `thingWordsOf`), `src/worlds/index.mjs` (loads `typed.json` into `world.raw.typed`), `src/dialog/index.mjs` (`set.typedFor(id, { things })`), `src/formalizer/completeness.mjs` (`completeSource`), `src/formalizer/routing.mjs`, `src/dialog/variants.mjs` (`voicesOf`, `ensembleOf`). **Build time, per world.** `node scripts/build-typed-worlds.mjs` writes `typed.json` for every world with patterns: the description `typedWorldFor` takes (`{ kinds: [{ type, words, keys: [{ key, value, many, enum, join }] }] }`, from the legend's `core.type` / `core.key` and, for a schema world (spec 21), its `schema.sop` kinds, words and attribute values), `known` (the words of the world's own lexicons, kinds, attributes and values), `args` (how request arguments are typed: `thing: phrase | open`, `place: place`; a world with its own reader (flag `reader`: the diagram) types none), `openSlots` (joins marked `open=yes`: the world takes names it does not know yet, they can be taught) and `knowledge` (the names and kinds of the knowledge things the keys join, from the catalog fixture's shards). `--check` exits 1 on a stale file; a test runs the same comparison. The session's things enter in two ways: `typedFor(id, { things })` rebuilds a typed world with them (memoised by the things, at most 8 kept), and, on the hot path, `thingWordsOf` adds the words of the world's current referents (names, kinds, labels: the executor's `referents`) as known words without a rebuild.

**What a typed world says about a reading (`unreadBy`, `PSYS2_COMPLETE_SRC=typed`, the default; `heuristic` = section 12's test).** A slot of a pattern fills a typed target read from the pattern's emit: `<type>|<key>` of a state fact or `<action>|<arg>` of a request. For each free-text slot (`words`, `noun`, `word`, `rest`): an open join is free; a request argument typed `place` (any slot class) or `phrase` (not `rest`: dictation stays free) must be made of words the world knows (its lexicons, kinds, values, joined things, session things, or an inflection of one) — every other content word is unread; a typed state key is checked by the library's `fitsSlot` (a joined key takes the name of one of its things, a closed set one of its values, a number a number, a name refuses a question word or a word the world reads as an act) and a refused slot's content words are unread. `rest` captures are checked only for `thing` and `value` keys. The words a `...` skip passed over stay unread as in section 12. So "put the boxes in a circle" on the avatar leaves "boxes" and "circle" unread (the avatar has no boxes and no circle place), while the diagram's arrangement reading is whole.

**Consequences.** The ones of section 12 (`PSYS2_COMPLETE=off|hold|ask`), now on typed unread words: arbitration by coverage (spec 20) picks per world its best-covered reading of the same tier (a catch-all handing the sentence to a world's reader does not hide a frame of that world that reads it whole), then orders the worlds by the library's decision; incomplete destructive readings are held; `ask` asks for any incomplete reading.

**The ensemble (`PSYS2_ENSEMBLE`; on by default until P6, now off: `PSYS2_ENSEMBLE=on`, section 15).** The voices of an unread or incomplete clause (form operators and canonicalise behind their switches, alternations, near words, slot types, run-on) run side by side; each proposes the complete readings its world accepts; the readings are pooled by their wires and `readEnsemble` decides with std.decide kind `ensemble` on the typed world of the clause's world (coverage, agreement = the voices that proposed the reading, cost, specificity). Two different readings with equal agreement and coverage: none (the library's `ask`), the clause keeps its reading. `segment.variant.ensemble` records `{ confidence, voices, readings }`. `off`: the earlier sequence (first accepted voice wins).

**Catch-all readings are a last resort (owner export 2026-11-14 22:43; `isCatchAll`, `LEFTOVER`, hook `readsWhole`).** "deseneaza o diagrama in forma de casa" ("Draw a diagram in house form") was read by `pat.diagram.sketch.read.frame` (`{pre:words} diagram {post:rest}`) with `arg.pre="Draw a"`, `arg.post="in house form"`, marked complete, and the reader drew one default circle. A pattern that swallows the words around one literal must not call itself complete. The rules:
1. *Leftover arguments are checked in every world.* The content words of a `pre` / `post` capture are unread unless the world knows them (`known`: its lexicons, kinds, values) or the world's own reader reads the whole clause (the executor's hook `readsWhole({ text, state })`; the diagram: a shape frame, a diagram request of a kind or topic, or any engine command or question beyond a bare default `create`; while its engine is still loading it is not asked). A world with its own reader (flag `reader`, `typed.json` `rawArgs: true`) has no typed arguments, so every free capture of its generated patterns is checked the same way.
2. *A catch-all reading* is a request whose every captured argument is raw text (`pre`, `post`, `rest`; `text` is the whole clause), or, in a `rawArgs` world, a reading that captured only free text (`seg.match.catchAll`). Half-read (unread words, no voice completed it), it is never executed, in `hold` as in `ask`: the reply is "I understood only part of ...: I did not use ...". The reader itself does not make a default thing for words it did not read (the diagram's `own` act: a frame the engine reads only as a bare `create` says which words it did not understand).
3. *The list* (tests/completeness.test.mjs computes it): art.draw / data.charts / diagram.sketch / docs.editor / geo.map / media.music / plan.seating / plan.timeline / ui.avatar `ask.why` (rest), data.charts / docs.editor / media.music `make.data.as.noun` / `make.data.verb` (rest: dictation, never unread outside a reader world), diagram.sketch `read.own`, `read.frame`, `read.frame.end`, `read.frame.shaped`, `read.frame.form`; a leftover next to a typed slot (checked by rule 1, not a catch-all): art.draw / diagram.sketch / ui.avatar `bare.mod`.

## 14. Foreign readings: a world other than the open tab's acts only on a full reading (2026-11-15; after the tab cut, ADR-010)

Code: `src/dialog/foreign-reading.mjs` (`reviewForeign`, `notFull`, `confidentTypo`, `moodOfClause`, `readsSpeaker`), called in `src/dialog/index.mjs` (`_sendCore`) right after `reviewSegments`, before anything is executed. **The decision is the library's reader contract (sopworld 0.120+, vendored 0.121):** every reading is handed to `readText(null, clause, { reader })` as one candidate `{ frame, unread, repaired }` built from the host segment; the library reads the clause's mood (`moodOf`: question, imperative, statement, fragment) and answers `complete` / `why`; a typed slot value is checked by `fitsSlot` (empty, an article alone, a single letter: a fragment). The host keeps only what the library cannot see: its patterns (`readsSpeaker`), its segments (ignored, respelled, reframed words), its tab table, the shared pattern lead (`ctx.lang.stripPolite`: `lang.lead.filler` then `lang.lead.polite`, so a wish is a request) and the confident-typo test of rule 4. `unreadStrict` from `src/formalizer/completeness.mjs` / `unreadBy(..., { strict: true })` in `src/formalizer/typed-world.mjs`; `seg.reframed` set by the rules formalizer when `readingVariants` reframed a gerund or a dropped subject as a command. Tests: `tests/foreign-reading.test.mjs`. Switch: `PSYS2_FOREIGN=off` (A/B only; default on). What the library does not have yet is asked in `questions.md` (Q22 [LIB]).

**Why.** Removing worlds (ADR-010) left their turns without a home, and surviving worlds took them on a keyword: the drawing drew 2 people for "I have two kids" and cats for "I like cats, dogs and horses", answered "hint" by respelling it "mint"; the music world answered "Reading", "No way" (way → say) and the Romanian article "o" (the note A). Nothing in the readings was wrong by the rules of sections 12 and 13: free text (`rest`) was dictation, a dropped "I" was a note, a respelling a courtesy. They are wrong only for a world the user is not in.

**1. Full reading.** A reading of a world that is not the open tab's world, has a tab of its own (the cross-cutting worlds without a tab and the ambient worlds are exempt) and is not named in the message is executed only if `readText` calls it complete: no word unread (the strict typed reading `unreadStrict`: like section 13, but a `rest` capture of a typed argument is typed too: dictation is free only in the tab's own world; and the ignored words), no repair (a spelling, scene or voice correction, a voice rewrite: `seg.variant` other than a run-on split, a `reframed` command, a best-effort or partial reading), not a fragment (rule 3), and no typed slot value `fitsSlot` refuses as empty or a fragment (an untyped slot: no empty value). Otherwise the reading becomes an unparsed clause (`seg.foreign = { world, why, pattern }`) and takes the honest path: the open tab world's own `outOfScope` refusal, else "I did not understand ...". A world without a tab context (no `tabWorld`: tests in chat-only mode) is not reviewed by this rule.

**2. Mood.** The mood is the library's `moodOf` of the clause (with its question mark, if one follows it) after the shared pattern lead is dropped (`ctx.lang.stripPolite`: a spoken filler of `lang.lead.filler` such as "i mean", then a polite request lead of `lang.lead.polite` such as "i want", "i would like", "i need", "can you": a wish is a request, its mood is the rest's; "i like" is not a polite lead: "I like cats" is a statement), read in lowercase: the rule is about the speaker, so only the library's first-person test counts (its second test, a capitalised name then an auxiliary, also takes "Here is my tune" and the diagram's "Done is red"; questions.md Q22 L5). A `statement` ("I have two kids", "I like cats", "my name is Ana") is not a command: a request reading of it is refused in every world, the open tab's included, and navigation too ("I like music" does not open the Music tab), unless the world reads statements in that frame (`readsStatement`): the pattern's own frame reads the speaker (an inline literal or an alternative of the world's own phrase macros starting with "i": "I have to tidy my room" is a to-do, "I am lost" asks for help; the shared `$lang.*` lead-ins do not count), or one of the pattern's own examples is a statement by the same mood (session.things "My dog is called Rex"). A non-request reading ("Tom has 5 apples" in kids.arith is a fact of a story) is not refused. The verbless pieces of the same list ("..., dogs and horses") go with the statement. A statement nobody read gets the reply "That sounds like something about you. I cannot keep facts about you here." with status `unparsed` (nothing was understood; a refused request reading is `unsupported`); there is no profile world since ADR-010. The hand list `lang.mood.statement` is gone: the library's mood replaces it.

**3. Fragment.** A clause the library reads as a `fragment` (one letter or one article alone: what "o", "a" leave after translation) is never read by a world other than the open tab's; the tab's own world keeps it (a bare note "A" on the Music tab).

**4. Guessed respellings are never a silent full reading, in any world (the open tab's too; reviewer 2026-11-15 01:35, real row r0296).** "face un caca" on the Draw tab ("make a caca") was read as "make a face" because the scene repair matched the stem "cac" of "caca" to the stem "fac" of the sun's face, and the drawing added a disc to the sun. A respelled content word (a correction of kind spell, scene or voice; not an abbreviation, not a stutter, not a correction into a function word) leaves the reading incomplete unless it is a **confident typo** (`confidentTypo`), all of: (a) the typed word is not a word the system knows (any world's vocabulary, the catalog's words, the bundled names: a real word is not a typo of another); (b) the slip distance (insert, delete, substitute, swap of two neighbours: 1 each; spaces ignored, so a split or a merge costs nothing) between the whole words, or between their stems when both stems keep 4 letters or more ("corcodilii" / "crocodiles"), is within the spelling budget of the typed word (1 up to 6 letters, 2 from 7, none under 3); (c) the first letter is kept, or the first two letters are swapped. A confident typo is executed and the reply names it ("(I read "hosue" as "house")", the existing correction line). A guess is not executed: the reading becomes an unparsed clause (`seg.foreign.guessed`), the reply says what was not understood ("I did not know the word: caca"), and the clause quoted is the user's own words. Two guesses are asked instead ("Did you mean ...", `suggestions`, before the tab world's `outOfScope` refusal and the world-aware explanation): a sound-alike of spoken input (kind `voice`, as RP3's weak sound-alikes are) and a real word read as another ("Clean the map up" read as "Clear the map up": geo.map r2n077 now expects the question). For the other tabs' worlds rule 1 already refuses every respelling.

**Measurement (spec 08 / `scripts/real-set.mjs`).** Every result file records `build` (git HEAD) and `tree`, a hash of the contents of every file the measurement depends on (`scripts/lib/measure-build.mjs`: src, worlds, knowledge, the judge, the set); `record` and `scripts/learning-curve.mjs --record` refuse a result whose tree is not the current one (`--allow-stale` to override, flagged), and `tests/real-result-fresh.test.mjs` fails when a committed default result is older than the tree it claims to measure. The judged rows that need a removed world are RETIRED (kept, `retired: { reason, tab }`, old check in `check_pre_cut`): their check is the clean refusal (no visible state change, none of the surviving tab worlds acts); they are not in the headline and are reported as a separate line. The runner shows a removed tab as the Chat (Talk) tab and reads the tab world before the turn. Owner-log checks that only forbid a reply also get `world_not` for the tab worlds that cannot own the request. `visibleState` ignores a world whose state is an empty object (a world that creates its state lazily has changed nothing the user sees).

**The headline measures what ships (P1, reviewer 2026-11-15 01:15).** `run` defaults to `--mode shipped`: the webchat's own settings (`docs/webchat/ui/settings.mjs` `DEFAULT_SETTINGS` through `applyReading`: the encoder formalizer with the dispatcher model through onnxruntime-node (`--ort` or `PSYS2_ORT`), `fastPath`, `worldAware: veto`, `nxStrategy: rules`, the matrix reasoner), the per-turn send options of `docs/webchat/app.mjs` (no deadline), `engine.prepareMeta()`, and the knowledge the page loads: the catalog at the pinned commit (`src/knowledge/catalog-pin.mjs`, checked out by `scripts/lib/catalog-pin.mjs`) layered over the bundled subset. `--mode rules` is the named diagnostic (the same with the rules formalizer only; `…-<reasoner>-rules.json`). Random choices are seeded: every session starts a generator from `REAL_SEED` and its session key (`Math.random`), and `Date` reads a fixed clock reset per session, so two runs of one tree give identical rows (`real-set.mjs diff <a> <b>` compares them without the time taken).
