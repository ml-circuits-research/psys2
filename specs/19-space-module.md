# 18 — Tab worlds and shared modules; the module `space`

Status: implemented (first version, 2026-11-13). Owner direction: the drawing world deals with drawings; where things are, which one is meant by its position and how to arrange them come from one place that other worlds can ask too; and there must not be more and more worlds competing for a message.

## 1. Two kinds of worlds

| Kind | What it is | Routing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tab world** | What the user does; one per tab (`tab=` in `world.sop`): art.draw, diagram.sketch, data.charts, geo.map, games.chess, media.music, docs.reader, user.profile. It has patterns, operations and state, and it competes for messages (spec 03 section 4, spec 16). | routing candidate |
| **Shared module** | A world, or a library of one, that tab worlds import and that never competes: no patterns of its own, no operations of its own, so it is never a routing target. `space`, `common.sense` (spec 15), the knowledge catalog lookup (spec 17), the follow-up handling of the dialogue layer (spec 11). | never a candidate |

Do **not** merge tab worlds into one: every phrase would compete with every other, which is the wrong-world problem. What several tabs need is a module they ask.

**Worlds that are really modules today** (found by the repository: no `tab=` and the patterns are used while another tab is active). They can stop being routing candidates of their own; what they offer is reached from the active tab or the conversation layer: `ui.help` (answers about the app, a conversation concern), `ui.shell` (opens tabs, settings: dialogue layer), `ui.avatar` (the avatar tab's commands, used from any tab), the `kids.*` worlds (`kids.arith`, `kids.shop`, `kids.time`, `kids.animals`, `kids.shapes`, `kids.tasks`, `kids.common`: small-talk and lessons reached from any tab), and the games with no `tab=` (`games.guess`, `games.hangman`, `games.tictactoe`: a candidate only while a game runs, spec 16 `no-game` veto). Moving them out of routing is a separate decision (it touches the dispatcher labels); WORLD-OF-WORLDS (`meta.worlds`) is where it is decided, with `kind=tab|module` on the world card. This spec only introduces the kind and the first module that is built as one from the start.

## 2. The module `space` (`worlds/space/`)

Files: `world.sop` (namespace `space`, status fixture, no patterns), `legend.sop` (the projection), `lexicon.sop` (the words, generated from the tables of `space.mjs` by `scripts/build-space-lexicon.mjs`; `tests/space.test.mjs` checks it is not stale), `space.mjs` (rules and arithmetic, browser-safe, no imports), `tests.sop` (a placeholder: it is tested through its hosts and by `tests/space.test.mjs`).

**Projection.** A host gives `space.item id= kind= x= y= w= h= z=` (the centre and the box, y grows downward, in the host's units) in `space.frame w= h= [step=]` (`step`: how far one plain move goes; default a tenth of the width). The module answers with selections (ids), target boxes (`{ id: { x, y [, w, h] } }`) and sentences; the host applies the boxes to its own objects (art.draw: `o.x`, `o.y`, `o.size`; diagram.sketch: engine `move ... at x,y` and `resize` nodes, so the engine's undo and rules still apply). The module never draws and knows nothing about houses or boxes: a host passes `kindOf(word) -> { kind, plural }` for the words that name its kinds (`*` means any kind).

**1. Selection by position and order** (`parseSel`, `select`): first, second, last (reading order: rows from the top, each row left to right), left / right / top / bottom (`the left one`, `leftmost`, `the second from the right`, `the two on the left`), middle (the median along the axis the group spreads on; with an even number there is no middle: the host asks), nearest to / next to X, between X and Y, biggest / smallest, "the other one" (not what the last action handled). With a kind ("the middle house") and without ("the one in the middle"). An ambiguous selection returns `{ ask, candidates }` with the candidates named by position ("There are 3: the left house, the middle house and the right house. Which one do you mean?"); the module never silently takes the first. A host that has context (a selected thing, the one thing just handled) uses it before asking.

**2. Relative moves** (`parseMove`, `planMove`): direction words, combined ("left and down" is both), each with an amount: default one step, a bit (0.5), a tiny bit (0.3), more / much (2.2), a lot (2.5), way / far (3), all the way (to the edge), by half (half the way to the edge). "further", "even more", "again" repeat the last move (`memory` returned by a move is kept by the host as `lastMove`; SCENE-NATURAL P2 calls into this). Boxes stay inside the frame.

**3. Placements by relation** (`parseRel`, `planPlace`): in, on, above, under, next to / near, left of, right of, between, around, in a corner (top left ...), the centre, at an edge, on the left half. The target box does not overlap another item unless the relation is containment (in, on, around); a fixed side slides along the side until it is free, "next to" tries the side the thing is on, then the others.

**4. Layouts** (`parseLayout`, `planLayout`): in a row, in a column, in a grid, in a circle, spread evenly, align (left, right, top, bottom, centres), same size, centre the group, swap two, tidy up (push overlapping boxes apart along the shorter way, keep the order). Free arrangements only: the diagram's layout engine stays for connected graphs (`arrange`, `align`, `distribute`, its score), and "make a better layout" maps to tidy up when nothing is connected and something overlaps.

**5. Questions** (`parseQuestion`, `answerQuestion`): "what is next to the house?", "which one is in the middle?", "is the sun above the tree?", "how far apart are the sun and the tree?" (touching, close, a bit apart, far apart; never pixels).

**6. Words.** The spatial words are `core.lexicon` groups of the module (`lex.space.ordinals`, `positions`, `sizes`, `counts`, `dirs`, `amounts`, `more`, `layouts`, `relations`); a host's patterns use them (`{d:lex.space.dirs}`) and a host lexicon extends them (`extends=$lex.space.amounts`). New phrasing for these words goes in the module, never in a host's patterns.

`interpret(text, ctx)` reads a whole sentence at once for a host that has no reading of its own; `ctx = { items, frame, kindOf, last, lastMove }`; the result is `{ type: 'move' | 'layout' | 'answer' | 'ask' | 'error', ids, boxes, say, memory, dest, kind, op }` or `null` when the sentence is not about places.

## 3. How a host uses it

- **art.draw** keeps its own reading of the verb and the thing (`move`, noun phrases with colour and parts). `parseNP` takes the spatial words from the module (`POSITIONS`, `ORDINALS`, `SIZES`, `COUNTS`); `resolveNP` hands the candidate list to `space.select` (a request that acts on the thing, `ask: true`, asks instead of taking the newest; callers that only look a thing up keep the old fallback and its note); `parseDest` reads the amount and the directions with `space.parseMove` and `applyDest` moves with `space.planMove`; the last move is kept in `lastMove` (for "further") and in `lastMoveInfo` (for "no, not the first one, the middle one": the op `draw.reselect` puts the things back and moves the one meant now, one request). New: the op `draw.spatial` (patterns `x.space.*`) for row / column / grid / circle / tidy up / swap / align and the questions. Ground-aware relations (a thing stands on the ground) stay in the host's `placeRelative`.
- **diagram.sketch** hands a moving or arranging sentence to `space.interpret` before the engine reads it (`spaceTry`, only for the actions move, align, arrange, distribute, respace, improve, swap, snap, fit): a selection by place, amounts, "further", a relation, "tidy up" of overlapping free shapes and "swap the first and the last" are applied as engine nodes; whatever the module cannot read (a shape named by its name, a layout of the engine) goes to the engine unchanged. The ops `diagram.reselect` and `diagram.spatial` (questions: is A above B, which is in the middle, how far apart; names go to the engine's `check` and `list`) are the same as in art.draw.

## 5. SPACE-2: surfaces, joining, knowledge layouts, consistency rules (2026-11-13)

**Surfaces.** An item may carry its real outline in the projection: `shape` (polygons as offsets from the centre) or `parts` (the union of part boxes as offsets from the centre); the box stays the bounding box. `overlaps`, `gap`, non-overlap placement (`planPlace`), tidy up and joining all use the surface when either item has one and fall back to the box otherwise (`surface`, `shaped`, `outlineOf` for plain geometric shapes). Hosts: art.draw passes the solid parts (rects, polygons, ellipses, turned and mirrored like the drawing) of composite and catalog things (built-in kinds stay boxes); diagram.sketch passes the outline of triangle, diamond, hexagon, star, circle and ellipse; ui.avatar passes the anchor box of a slot as `parts`. Placement still tries the box-clean positions first, so a request that worked before gives the same answer; the surface only opens positions the boxes would refuse, and it decides tidy up (two triangles whose boxes overlap in an empty corner are already tidy).

**Joining.** When a placement or a move ends close to another item (within 4.5 % of the frame diagonal) and the relation implies contact, the two are snapped so they touch (a 1.5 px hair of air) without overlapping (`settle` slides along the line to the neighbour until the outlines meet, `eject` lifts out a thing that sank in). Contact is implied by: on / onto / on top of, against, the words touching / joined / attached, and a pair that common sense says belongs together (`KNOWLEDGE.supports`: a roof on walls, a cup on a table, a wheel on a road). "Don't join them", "leave a gap", "with a gap", "keep them apart", "apart", "not touching" (`contactWish`) switch it off for that request and keep a gap (5 % of the frame width, `apartAfter` for hosts that place by themselves). Joins are reported in plain words: "I set the roof on the walls." (on, under, against). `joinAfter` is the entry for hosts with their own move (art.draw `move`).

**Layouts from knowledge.** New ops, all through `parseLayout` / `planLayout` / `interpret`: `around` (chairs around a table, "in a circle around the table": evenly on a ring, each touching the reference, spread out until the movers do not overlap; the movers are the named kind, or the kinds `KNOWLEDGE.around` lists for the reference), `height` (a row sorted by size on one baseline; "from the tallest to the shortest" reverses), `stack` (on top of each other, touching: what supports sits lower, else the bigger one), `face` (rank from the top: brows, eyes and ears, nose, mouth; mirrored pairs either side of the middle: `KNOWLEDGE.face`). The facts are data: `KNOWLEDGE` in `space.mjs` and `common.sense.supports / around / facepart` in `worlds/common.sense/kb.sop` (a test keeps them equal; a host may pass its own in `ctx.knowledge`). A single thing "around" another stays the host's move (it wraps it).

**Consistency rules.** Declared once as `RULES` (`inside-frame`, `no-overlap`, `on-ground`, `face-limits`; listed as `space.rule` rows in `worlds/space/rules.sop`, generated with the lexicon by `scripts/build-space-lexicon.mjs`) and applied by `enforce(items, boxes, frame, { move, contain, ground, limits })` to every placement, move and layout `interpret` answers: boxes are clamped into the frame; a layout or placement never leaves a new overlap (containment relations excepted), a plain move that would newly overlap stops short; supported kinds (`ground = { top, kinds }`) are not lifted off the ground by a layout and a move that lifts one is done and said; slot `limits` clamp a face slot. SOP Lang can state the rule rows and the facts but not the geometry (sopworld has no polygon, ordering, median or fixed-point calc, see questions.md Q2), so the geometry stays host functions in `space.mjs`.

**Tests and eval.** `training/eval/space-set2.mjs`: 60 seeded cases (surface keep / tidy / place, joining on / apart / move, knowledge layouts) run by `node scripts/eval-space.mjs` after the 150 base cases (150/150 in both reasoners, 60/60); `tests/space.test.mjs` has the SPACE-2 unit tests. Not done: wiring `enforce` ground limits for art.draw's own placements (they use `placeRelative`), and an avatar layout command for the face order (the avatar keeps its own slot limits).

## 6. SPACE-3: pieces, relations kept as facts, the stable solver, layouts from `k.layouts` (2026-11-13, worker-tasks 17:40)

Files: `worlds/space/pieces.mjs` (layers 1 and 2), `worlds/space/solver.mjs` (layer 3), `src/knowledge/layouts.mjs` (the `k.layouts` reader), `tests/space2-pieces.test.mjs`, `scripts/exp-space.mjs` (E-S1 to E-S6), `scripts/measure-ledger-space.mjs` (the failure-ledger classes before / after). All browser-safe, plain data in and out, deterministic, every loop bounded and ticking the turn budget.

**Pieces.** `pieceFrom(item)`: extent (box), `shape` (the convex hull of the part points of a k.objects thing, `outlineFromParts`), layer (`behind` / `front` from z), named anchors (`top bottom left right centre` plus the anchors knowledge gives the kind: seat of a swing, surface of a lake, chin of a head, ports of a box; `ANCHOR_TABLE` is the fallback, a host passes the table of `k.faces` / `k.objects`), `parent` (a piece may contain pieces: a face holds eyes, a lake holds a duck), `container`, `region`, `pinned`. `attachAt(piece, anchor, target, targetAnchor)` gives the centre that puts one anchor on another (a hat on the top of the head, an arrow on a port). `faceLayout` is the face API for ui.avatar (slots by the order of a face through `planLayout('face')`).

**Relations as facts.** `createStore()` is `{ facts, seq }`, JSON-safe, kept in the host's state and snapshotted by its undo (art.draw: `state.rels`). A fact is `{ rel, a, b, source, tier, seq }` with `rel` one of on, in, attached_to, next_to, left_of, right_of, above, in_front_of, behind, aligned_with, same_size_as, apart_from, inside_frame. `propose` ranks conflicts by the delegation tiers said > focus > scene > knowledge > default (equal tier: the newer wins); a weaker fact against a stronger one is refused with a plain reason ("I will not do that: the tree is kept apart from the house (you said so)."). `apart_from` is a `said` fact and stays until `retract` ("you can join them"). `inferFacts` is the rule set (`join-near`: near pieces that belong together by knowledge, supports or part-of, are `on` / `attached_to`; `in-container`), each fact with its rule as source, never against an `apart_from`. `carriedBy` / `movePiece`: moving a piece moves what is on, in or attached to it (transitively, and its child pieces), never what is merely next to it; the carried piece that ends off its support loses the fact and the reply says so. `checkMove`: a thing is not pushed into what it stands on ("the cat can not go lower: it is on the house."). `holdsFact` tests a relation on the present geometry; `why` says a fact with its source.

**Solver.** `solve({ pieces, facts, frame, layout, ids, pinned, edges, mentioned, maxSteps, only, names })`: (0) every piece that may move is brought inside the frame; (1) the named layout (a generator or the slots of a `k.layouts` entry); (2) the relation constraints, weakest tier first so the strongest wins, a piece the frame stops makes the other give way; (3) overlap removal by the smallest move (broad phase on boxes, then the real surfaces; a move that breaks a fact is the last resort; what carries something moves it too); (4) whole pixels, then the check (every fact, the frame, no new overlap between a mover and anything). On a failed check, a refused relation or an exhausted step budget the answer is `{ ok: false, boxes: {} }` and a sentence naming the relation or the pair: the host keeps the last valid state. Pieces the user placed are `pinned`: they never move (stability). Generators: row, column, grid, ring, corners (a corner taken by something that stays is skipped; more things than corners use the middles of the edges then the centre; "at the top" keeps the top ones), stack, height, face, landscape (sky above, ground, water), flow (left to right) and tree (top down) over `edges`, scatter (a hash of the id), and any layout that is only slots.

**Layouts are knowledge.** `createLayouts({ source })` reads `k.layouts/shards/layouts.sop` through the catalog source (the generic knowledge reader is not on main: only `load` changes when it is). Format: `@layout.<id> k.layouts.layout name= synonym= generator= sort= when=`, or slots as data `slot="<fx> <fy>"` (fractions of the frame, filled in the order of `sort`: reading, height, size, none) with optional `rel="slot1 left_of slot2"`. `find("like a family photo")` picks by name or synonym (longest wins). The local `FALLBACK` is used only when the catalog has none. `tests/fixtures/k.layouts.sop` is the local fixture.

**Hosts.** art.draw: placing by relation and the contact words keep facts; a move carries what rides on the thing, says what it dropped, refuses to push a thing into its support or to bring together what the user asked to keep apart; a layout is a move destination (`move the fish in different corners`, `in a row`) through `solve` with everything else pinned. diagram.sketch: around, by height, stack and corners come from the module (`spaceTry`), no pattern of its own. Not wired: arrows attached at ports in the diagram engine (it routes its own; `attachAt` and the box ports are ready), the avatar's own slot placing (ui.avatar belongs to ONE AVATAR; the API is `anchorsOf`, `attachAt`, `faceLayout`).

**Limits.** Outlines are convex hulls (an L-shaped house is over-approximated); joining depends on knowledge having supports / part-of pairs (the fallback list is small; kinds without a pair never join); the solver is a smallest-move heuristic, not a physics engine: a crowded frame (about 200 small pieces at 30 % fill) can end with "I could not separate ..." and the last state kept.
