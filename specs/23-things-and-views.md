# 22 — Things, aspects and views: transfer of knowledge between worlds (TRANSFER, first slice)

Implementation: `worlds/session.things/` (module world: `things.mjs` the store, `view.mjs` the view wrapper, `ops.mjs` the operations, patterns that read what the user teaches), `src/knowledge/learned.mjs` (session.learned), a `view` declaration in a schema (spec 21, `acts.view`), thin hooks in `worlds/acts/acts.mjs` (`before`, `after`, `send` with a refusal reason) and `src/interpreter/index.mjs` (`ctx.knowledge` is the catalog with the session's learned wires overlaid). Tests: `tests/things.test.mjs`. Experiments: `scripts/exp-transfer.mjs` (T1, T2).

## 1. Why
Each tab kept its own things, and moving one to another tab was code per pair (Talk card to drawing, to diagram, to map). What a thing is does not depend on the tab that shows it. One store of things per session; a tab is a view of it.

## 2. A thing
`{ id, kind, name, label, attrs, parts, origin, shown, aspects }`. The identity (`id`) is kept across views. `kind` is a word of the knowledge ("hat", "table", "city"); `attrs` hold what the view that made it knows (colour, seats, place, name); `parts` is the picture in the 0-100 frame when it has one; `origin` is the view that made it, `shown` the views that hold it. The state of the world `session.things` is the store; it lives in the session and is saved with it.

## 3. Aspects
visual, spatial, relational, numeric, textual, geographic, temporal, social. `aspectsOf` reads, strongest first: `knowledge` (what the catalog says about the kind: parts = visual + spatial, `k.geo.*` = geographic + spatial, `k.time.*` = temporal, `k.people.*` = social + visual, `k.faces.*` = visual + spatial, `k.animals.*` and `k.units.*` and commonsense `usually_count` = numeric), `learned` (what the session was taught, and what the kind is learned to be a kind of lends), `schema` (`aspect=` of an `acts.kind`), `attribute` (an attribute the thing carries: colour is visual, a number is numeric, `seats`, `place` ... by a table marked TEMPORARY until `k.attributes` arrives). Every named thing has `textual` and `relational`: it can be listed and linked. Each aspect keeps its source, so a reply can say why.

## 4. Views
A schema world declares `acts.view` (`aspects=`, `word=`); the generator gives it two operations: `<ns>.view` (its declaration) and `<ns>.adopt` (take a thing: the act `make` of the executor with the thing's kind and name, then `change` for each attribute the schema has for that kind; one undo step per act as always). A world that is not a schema world (Talk) writes the two operations itself. The gate of `things.show` is two questions asked in this order, and each refusal is said in plain words:
1. the thing has at least one aspect the view shows ("A hat has no look to show in..." never reaches the user for a thing that has one);
2. the view has a kind for it, read at the knowledge tier: an unknown word that the knowledge says is a kind of one of the view's kinds is read as that kind ("a waiter is a kind of person", "A waiter is a kind of guest. I added guest Sam.").
The act `send` of every view (and "send it to X" of Talk) is this one operation; there is no code per pair of views. A view that does not hold the thing named in a `send` hands the words to the store, which knows every thing of the session.

## 5. Who writes what
The view that made a thing owns its kind, name, label and parts. Any view may change an attribute by an act: after every request a view publishes its things (`things.sync`, the whole list), and a view other than the origin changes an attribute only where it differs from what that view published last (`seen`), so a stale copy never overwrites what another view changed; the first publication of a view overwrites nothing. A thing that no view shows any more is dropped.

## 6. session.learned
`parseTeach` reads a taught sentence in six forms: "X is a kind of Y" / "a X is a Y", "my X is called N" / "I have a X called N", "X can / cannot V", "X V" with V an ability (seed list, TEMPORARY), "X live in Y", "X is made of Y". Each becomes a wire of the knowledge format (`@learned.N k.words.sense ... is_a=` / `k.commonsense.thing ... can= cannot= at= made_of=` / `session.learned.own kind= name= owner=user`, `core.source="user"`) in the state of `session.things`. `withLearned(catalog, read)` returns the catalog with those wires overlaid (same API: `peek`, `thing`, `wires`, plus `own(kind)` and `ancestors(word)`): a taught kind is drawn as the kind it is a kind of, a taught ability is in the commonsense record, a thing of the user is a thing of its kind under its name. The overlay belongs to the session; the shared catalog is never changed. A form that nothing reads is a ledger row, not a pattern.

## 7. Known limits of this slice
- Charts, the document, the timeline and the map are views (sections 8 and 9). Draw and diagram are not views yet; Talk's `send` to draw and diagram is still code per pair (about 20 lines) and goes when those worlds are views.
- Facts about animals and pets taught in a sentence that `kids.animals` also reads ("ducks float", "fish live in water", "my dog is called Rex") are stored by that world as its own wires and are not seen by other views: the same sentence has two readers and the one with the private store wins the routing. Unifying them (statement wires of every world visible to the overlay) is the next decision, not a pattern.
- `k.attributes` is not in the catalog; the attribute table in `things.mjs` and the ability seed in `learned.mjs` are marked TEMPORARY.
- A view holds a copy, not the shared object: another view sees a change at its next show or sync.

## 8. Selections, the chart and the document as views, the timeline (2026-11-13)
**Selection.** "a chart of what I drew", "a document about the things in the plan", "a chart about the current discussion" are three patterns of `session.things` (`signal=true`, so they beat the open tab's own reading; the view word is read from `lex.session.things.views`, TEMPORARY, so that "number of items on my list" is not a request for a view) that emit the one operation `things.show` with `arg.thing` = the selection and `arg.to` = the view. `select(state, phrase, views)` (things.mjs) reads the selection: the whole session ("the discussion", "what we did", "everything"), what one view holds (the verb names it: "what I drew" is the drawing, "in the plan" the seating plan), else a name or a kind. A world that holds things without being a view (the drawing board until it is one) is bridged: its referents, as `session.dialogue` offers them (`ctx.sessionFacts()`, a hook like `ctx.dialogueFocus`, also on the dry-run fork), enter the store under the world's id and keep their identity. The M1 `sessionContent` (a rewrite of the sentence into data for one world) is deleted; `facts()` stays as the offer.
**A view that takes a set.** A view declaration may say `many: true` (one `adopt` request for the whole selection: one step to undo, and the view sees the set), `counts: true` (every thing is countable: one more of its kind, so the gate of aspects does not refuse it) and `facts: true` (it also takes the topics, games and places of the discussion).
**Charts** (`data.charts.view` / `.adopt`, 26 hand-written lines in `chart.mjs`, exported by `own.mjs`: a view that takes a set is not yet a declaration of the schema, `acts.view` has no `many` / `counts`): numeric aspect, `counts`, `many`; a thing is one of its kind, a thing that carries a number of its own (value, count, amount, price, population) is a bar of that number under its name; further things are merged into the chart made from things.
**Document** (since 2026-11-14 a schema world, spec 21 section 12; `docs.editor.view` / `.adopt` and the act make-document `doc.make`, 63 lines, are in `editor.mjs` and exported by `own.mjs`): textual aspect, `many`, `facts`. "A document about ..." is a new document, one section per view the things come from (a list), then Topics, Games, Places from the facts; a thing sent alone is added to the section of its view in the open document. `doc.make` (title, `content` text and/or `sections`, one request, one undo step; patterns "make a document called X with the text Y" / "... saying Y") fixes what M1 found: the editor could not take a document and its text in one request.
**Timeline** (`worlds/plan.timeline`, T6-lite): a world from a schema alone (52 lines of `schema.sop`, `acts.view aspects=temporal`, kind `moment` with `aspect=temporal`, attribute `when`); everything else is generated; 30 lines of tab plugin draw the moments in a row by `when` (`docs/webchat/ui/tabs/timeline.mjs`). A thing with a time (a weekday, a month, a season: `k.time` in the knowledge) is taken as a moment even when the schema has no word for its kind (`adopt`: a kind this world declares with an aspect the thing has); a bicycle is refused plainly ("A bicycle has no time to show in the timeline").
**Not yet:** the chart, the document and the timeline take things but do not publish things of their own (no matrix row is made in them); a thing's time is not read from `k.time` (`when` defaults to 1 and is set by "set Monday time to 2"); the acts of drawing are not read in the selection phrases ("a chart of the sun I drew").

## 9. The map as a view, an act verb that a free-text reading swallowed, common sense at the knowledge tier (2026-11-13)
**The map** (`geo.map.view` / `.adopt`, 32 lines in `ops.mjs`): geographic aspect (from `k.geo` through the knowledge tier, `aspectsOf`), `many`. A thing is a pin where the map knows its place: by name or kind in the data of the world, else by the `lat` and `lon` it carries; what has none is said plainly ("A moon has no position on Earth to show in the map ..." from the gate of aspects, "I do not know where X is" from the map). One request for a selection, one undo step, markers and focus as the world's own commands make them. Selection phrases: "show it on the map" (`session.things.select.it`: the last thing, any view) and "put the places we talked about on the map" (`select.places`: the noun names an aspect, `ASPECT_NOUN_TEMPORARY` in things.mjs, so "places" are the things with a geographic aspect). Talk's `send` to the map and its pattern and phrase (`lang.talk.tomap`) are deleted: the map is reached by `things.show` like every other view.
**Act verb swallowed** (`src/formalizer/rules/index.mjs`, after the decline loop): a free-text reading whose wildcard slot starts with the verb of an act (`$acts.verb.<act>`) that another world's act pattern, whose action ends in that act, reads in the same sentence yields to the act. "Send table 1 to the chart": the chart's data pattern took "send table" as its data and won as the named world; the seating plan's `send` is now read and `things.show` puts the table in the chart. No phrase is listed; the closed verb group is the act's own.
**Common sense** (`src/knowledge/commonsense.mjs`, T3): `habitat(knowledge, word)` is the one lookup of where a kind of thing usually is (`k.scenes` place first, then `k.commonsense` at, taught or catalog, then the kind-of chain), `adviceFor` and `habitatSentence` say it. Talk answers with it ("You find it on the ground", for the catalog and for what the user taught, no longer only the latter); the drawing board asks it for a kind it has no row for (`anchorOf` fallback, and the advice "A snowman is usually found on the ground, not in the sky." in both reasoners, where `ctx.rulesAdvise` says whether the soft rules of common.sense speak themselves, so a kind is never advised twice); `parseTeach` reads "a house stands on the ground", "trains stay on the ground", "boats float on water" as habitat facts. The place words and their broad zones (`ZONES`) are TEMPORARY until `k.commonsense` has a place hierarchy.
**Measured** (`node scripts/exp-transfer.mjs --only t3`, both reasoners identical): 20 facts x drawing, diagram, Talk = 60 cells: 27 right, 14 not applicable said plainly, 19 wrong (before: 16 / 14 / 30). Talk 19 of 20 (before 9), drawing 8 right of 15 that apply (before 7), the diagram 0 of 11: it has no notion of place or containment (a connection is an arrow with no meaning), so nothing asks the knowledge tier there; this is a ledger class, not a patch. The other misses are in the ledger by cause: the drawing grammar does not read "<kind> in the <place>" for a kind of the catalog or a taught kind (4), "in the water" is read as the bottom band and the place word is lost (3), another world (kids.animals) reads "ducks float" first and keeps it private (1).

## 10. Taught facts at the knowledge tier for every view (2026-11-13, T2)
A taught fact reaches a view only through `ctx.knowledge` (the catalog with the session's overlay); a view has no code for it. Causes of the T2 misses and their fixes: (1) the act reader drops "my" from the role ("add my sister" gives `thing=sister`), so the view's own-thing lookup never ran: the view now also reads whose it is from the sentence (`req.text`); (2) `ancestors()` of the overlay followed only word senses: it now also follows what the catalog records say a word is (`is=` of a role, `is_a=` of a kind) and, last, the figure it is drawn as (`drawing=person`), so a sister is a guest with no table of words; (3) a bare "ducks float" was read as the question "can ducks swim" by a generated pattern of kids.animals (a question pattern with every lead optional): the teach form for a noun and an ability is a statement of priority 11 (statements are skipped for questions, and a noun slot cannot start with "do"). Remaining misses are in the failure ledger with cause: the tab world not claiming its own verb for a kind it cannot use ("put on a boat" is drawn by the drawing world), and catalog entries missing ("friend", and "child/man/woman is a person" as an is-a, not only `drawing=person`).
