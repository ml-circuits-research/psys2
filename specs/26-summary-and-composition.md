# 26. Summary and composition (host side of `std.summarize` and `std.compose`)

Status: `std.summarize` (0.61) and a first `std.compose` (0.64/0.65) are released and wired. The composition runs over the bundled catalog subset (k.interest, k.genres, k.animals, k.geo). The five document types map to the published `k.genres` genres, but **sopworld 0.65 reads `move=` on a genre and the published genres have parts and steps**, so it throws on them: only the essay is composed (by the library's default moves, the genre named as not applied); the other four types are declined with that reason until the library reads parts and steps.

## Rule
No LLM, no local generator, no fallback text. Summaries and compositions come from `std.summarize` / `std.compose` through `src/dialog/textgen.mjs`. Without them the adapters are `available: false`, the UI that needs them is not shown (the Talk paragraph, the Documents buttons and form (the editor tab)), and the commands decline plainly ("I cannot write compositions yet: the library has no composer (std.compose) in this build.").

## Interface (as implied by the reviewer's block of 2026-11-14 14:45; to be confirmed with the library)
- **Real `std.summarize` (0.61):** `summarizeLog(entries, { parentOf, length })` takes `[{ act, kind, failed?, changed? }]` and returns FRAMES `[{ act, kind, count, kinds?, failed?, salience }]` (aggregated by act and kind, siblings generalised through `parentOf`; `length` line / paragraph = 3 / bullets = all). `summarizeText(text, { n, stop, redundancy })` returns `{ sentences: [{ text, score, at }], picked: [text] }` (extractive; language-free). The library writes no English.
- **Host rendering (TEMPORARY, until the language world renders frames):** `renderFrames` in `src/dialog/textgen.mjs` turns frames into "You drew two trees and a house; one request was not understood or could not be done." Entries come from what the worlds EXECUTED (`executedActs` in `src/dialog/conversation.mjs`): per supported answer, the operation that ran (`op.art.draw.make` gives world and act) and, for each thing it added or changed, the kind the world's own state records. Turns that were refused or not understood have no entries and are only counted; the user's words never reach the paragraph. Taught facts are narrated only when the knowledge world stored a relation in English (`narratable`: `word is_a kind`, `your kind is called name`); other stored facts (free attributes of an unknown word) are not narrated. Failed frames carry a salience bonus, so the host asks for all frames (`bullets`), counts the failures in the sentence and shows the best three that worked. The paragraph has at most three sentences: what was done (frames), the worlds used (every one, by id), what was taught (every fact). `parentOf` is not passed yet (no generalisation of siblings; a follow-up from the knowledge catalog).
- **Real `std.compose` (0.71, pinned 0.72):** `compose(world, topic, { genre, size: { sentences }, seed, realise })` over a World holding the knowledge. With a published `k.genres` genre the library lays the text out by the genre: `{ genre, register, topic, seed, parts: [{ part, heading?, relation?, moves: [frame] }], story?, short? }`; a frame is `{ part, move, takes, tense, cites | recalls | element+value, fact?, text?, template? }`. The genre decides the parts and their repeats (the solver picks them), `size.sentences` is a budget (the chapters and subchapters of a request do not apply to a genre). Without a `realise` option the library realises the frames through the discourse templates of `w.lang.en` (`discourseRealiser`: a template of the frame's move and register whose slots all fill; no sentence twice while another fits); a frame with no fitting template has no text and is listed in `short`. Stories are told from a plan of `k.story` (`readStory`, `storyPlan`). Every frame that states a claim cites its wire; a composition is deterministic by its seed.
- **Host mapping (`createComposer` in `src/dialog/textgen.mjs`, `src/dialog/compose-world.mjs`, `src/lang/en-generate.mjs`):** the World is the bundled catalog subset (`knowledgeWorld`: k.genres, k.interest, k.animals, k.geo, and `src/knowledge/bundled-compose.mjs`, generated by `scripts/build-bundled-compose.mjs`: k.story, w.lang.en's discourse and act templates, the irregular plurals, auxiliaries and prepositions, k.scenes, k.actions, k.people, the names of k.objects, what k.commonsense, k.attributes and k.parts say about the animals). Type -> `genre` when the world holds `k.genres.genre name=<type>`. The words asked set the sentence budget (`words / 16`). The sentences are the library's: the host passes only what the language owns (`englishOf(lib, world).realiser(seed)`: `np`, `clause`, `inflect`, and a `fill` that says a fact only when it is about the topic); there is no host template and no host frame renderer for composition. A text that claims nothing (no sentence citing a wire) is declined: "I know nothing about X in my knowledge yet". The result is memoised by (type, topic, size, seed) in the composer, and `doc.compose` derives its seed from the session and the request, so the engine's dry run and real run write the same document. Chapters of the document are the parts that say something.
- `checkComposition` (frames): at least one part that says something, every written sentence free of placeholders, every claim (a move that takes a fact) citing a wire, no fact stated twice, and at least one claim (a text about nothing is declined). Counts are clamped: words 50-5000.

## Document types (amendment 2026-11-14 15:00)
`type` is one of `essay`, `scientific-article`, `presentation`, `story`, `report` (`DOC_TYPES` in the adapter holds only the defaults of chapters / subchapters / words: essay 3/2/600, scientific article 4/2/1200, presentation 5/0/300, story 3/0/500, report 3/2/800). The type's **procedure and style come from knowledge** (`k.genres`, `k.story`, `k.procedures`) through the library's `genre` option; the host passes the genre name only when the knowledge holds it. No free random: a random story would be "by the book" (the story method).
Commands: "write a short story", "make a presentation about the moon with 5 sections" (sections = chapters), "write a scientific article about bees, 3 chapters", plus the earlier essay forms. The words essay / composition / text / piece = essay; article / scientific article = scientific-article; presentation / slideshow; story / tale; report (`lex.docs.reader.doctypes`). The form has a Type select.

## Host
1. **Talk, "what you did"**: `refreshSummary` (`docs/webchat/app.mjs`) adds `paragraph` from `summarizer.turns(...)` (at most 3 sentences) above the structured list (`src/dialog/conversation.mjs` `summarize`, unchanged). No paragraph while unavailable.
2. **Act "summarise"** (through `summarizeText`): `doc.digest` of docs.editor (the Documents tab; it moved from docs.reader at the tab cut, `specs/09-documents.md`): `summarize the following: <text>` (pasted text) and `condense the document` (the open document, k sentences). The reply is "In short: ..." (no sentence citation any more: the editor has no sentence index).
3. **Composition**: `doc.compose` of docs.editor with roles `topic`, `chapters`, `subchapters`, `words`. Commands (patterns in `worlds/docs.editor/patterns.extra.sop`, kinds in `lex.docs.editor.compkinds`): "write an essay about volcanoes with 3 chapters of 2 subchapters, about 600 words", "write a composition about anything", "write an essay about volcanoes", "... of 400 words". The Documents tab form (topic, chapters, subchapters, size) sends one of these commands, so the form and the command are the same path. Output replaces the open document of the editor (title, a heading per chapter and subchapter, a paragraph per subchapter); each paragraph keeps the source wires of its claims in `from`, so "why" names them.
4. Wiring: the engine installs the adapters in `globalThis[Symbol.for('psys2.textgen')]` when the library is loaded (`installTextGen`); the world operations (`worlds/docs.editor/textgen.mjs`) read it like the budget. The browser host creates its own adapters for the buttons (`host.textGen()`).

## Size and long topics (owner request 2026-11-15)
The owner asked for 5,000 words and got a few short paragraphs: `std.compose` writes about ONE topic from the facts that name it (a dozen for an animal, a score for a country), whatever the budget. The host now plans the rest from the knowledge (`src/dialog/compose-plan.mjs`, `widen` in `src/dialog/textgen.mjs`); nothing is padded.
- **Related topics** (`relatedTopics`): the names the topic's own facts give (`out`: what a penguin eats, a country's neighbours and capital; the values of verb-like keys such as `can` / `cannot` are acts and never topics), the names whose facts give the topic (`in`: the cities of a country, the groups it is a member of; an act of `k.actions` that names it links nothing), and its siblings of the same narrow kind (`sib`: the same `class`, `continent`, `region`, `group` of an animal, country or city; at most 25, those sharing more of the topic's (key, value) pairs first, beyond the first ten only those sharing two). A name whose facts are all acts or roles is no topic. Grouped by relation, `out` before `in` before `sib`; names a long topic asked for and keys it named come first.
- **A section per related topic**: a `### <name>` subheading, opened by the linking fact said by the library's `clauseOf` (projected on the subject and the one key: "The penguin eats krill.", cited to its wire), then the related topic's own facts (its subject wires of one kind: Luxembourg the country, not also the city; no act of the same name), the most interesting first by the library's `interestOf`, each said by `clauseOf` in sentences of two keys, never a (key, value) the section already said, never a sentence or a fact said anywhere before. Codes and coordinates are not narrated (`QUIET_KEYS`, passed as the library's `quiet` to the main composition too). Sections of one relation form a chapter (`## The penguin eats krill, squid and fish` by `clauseOf` for `out`, the names joined for the others), placed before the closing part of the genre (conclusion, summary, next steps, questions).
- **The size**: sections are added while the words written are under 95% of the words asked (at most 60 tried); the sentence budget of the main composition stays `words / 16`. When the knowledge runs out first the reply says so: "I could write about 2,060 words about the penguin from what I know (you asked for 5,000); I did not pad it with repetition." A story is told from one plan (`k.story`) and is not widened. Cost: about 90-180 ms for 5,000 words in Node (the related sections do not call `std.compose`: composing each with it took 40-160 ms and chose among the same facts).
- **A long topic** (`readLongTopic`; `isLongTopic`: more than four words or a sentence break): split into phrases (sentence ends, commas, and / but / because ...), the known things found in each (up to three-word names, other spellings and plurals from `core.word`, acts and roles ignored), and the words that name a fact key (eat -> `eats`, kept only when the topic or its relations have that key). The subject is the known name said most often (a subject of the composer first), the rest are subtopics: a subtopic linked to the subject by a fact is written as a section or counted as used; one that nothing links is not written and is said ("I know school and eiffel tower, but nothing that links them to France, so I left them out"); the phrases nothing was taken from are quoted back ("I found nothing I know in \"Also something about climate change\""). Nothing found at all is declined with the reason.
- **The form and the command**: the Documents form's topic is a text area that grows with the text (up to 320 px); a long topic is sent whole, in quotes, as `write an essay of <n> words about the following: "<text>"` (patterns `pat.docs.editor.compose.long` / `compose.long.size`, a `{topic:rest}` slot; the quotes keep its sentences from being read as requests of their own), a short one as before. The size is always sent (it was dropped when the form had no subchapters; `pat.docs.editor.compose.sections.size` reads "with 3 chapters, about 600 words"). A long topic typed without quotes is cut into clauses by the turn's clause splitting first (the long-message work, F) and only its first sentence reaches the composer.
- **Measured** (`node scripts/measure-compose.mjs --sizes`, essay, seed 7). Before: every size gave the same text: penguin 127 words / 4 chapters / 10 sentences, France 84 / 3 / 5, elephant 113 / 4 / 9. After (words, sections, sentences, repeated sentences, sentences traced to a wire): penguin 500: 485, 10, 53, 0, 51; 2,000: 1,923, 30, 219, 0, 217; 5,000: 2,060, 32, 235, 0, 233. France 500: 519 / 10; 2,000: 1,938 / 38; 5,000: 2,202 / 43. Elephant 500: 481 / 10; 2,000: 1,954 / 28; 5,000: 2,129 / 31. The two untraced sentences of each are the genre's framing ("Everyone has heard of X, yet few know the details", the conclusion's restatement). Repeated 4-grams grow with the subject named at the start of each sentence ("The seagull has a ...").
