The first landing isolates an unchanged historical diagram reader under `training/sop-bert/upstream`; it does not add an application reader, execute operations or enable a preview.

Reproduction must bind source, model, data split and evaluation protocol. The historical 83.8% refers to ac33631's model and the original 585-message held-out split. The current upstream all-data model has seen that split and cannot stand in for it. Source/model hashes, original split generation, per-message outcomes and exact runtime/platform metadata accompany the result. A mismatch is reported as a mismatch, without modifying cases or thresholds to make the headline agree.

The harness uses the original browser decoder, original native head layout and context rule, and original value-based matching/canonicalization of requested actions. An unexpected Rules fallback or model/source mismatch fails the run. Right, wrong, not-done and extra actions remain separate; precision of execution includes extras in its denominator. This execute-all historical measurement is not the later calibrated execution gate and is not new-path acceptance.

General retargeting is separate work. Its labels consume reviewed published request contracts, not provisional gold. Annotation conversion must preserve immutable source messages and report unrepresentable structures rather than silently dropping them. A roundtrip check is needed before training. Calibration data selects the execution threshold; the final unseen holdout measures it and cannot supply labels, rule corrections or threshold tuning. The model proposes a reading which resolution must verify in the instance; it cannot bypass the symbolic/evidence gates or claim an effect from inference alone.

## Label representability audit

`audit-labels.py` preserves every request from the three historical populations and uses the original class-target method itself. `audit-decode.mjs` replays gold logits through the frozen decoder/tokenizer, without trained weights. Given annotated spans isolate label/decode failures; the original cut labels are checked separately. No nearest-row substitution is allowed for a missing or mismatched span. Canonical equality additionally requires matching request type and no extra output keys. Per-request differences, unsupported spans, changed cut boundaries and all denominators remain in the result.

`--require-exact` makes representational loss a failed training prerequisite (exit 2 after evidence is written). The known frozen baseline fails this new diagnostic gate; publishing the audit does not claim that baseline labels are suitable for new training. The immutable baseline is not patched to pass. General retargeting must distinguish a legitimate value such as `none` from an absence sentinel, and must report values outside its representable schema rather than silently turning them into absence.

## Supported graph label codec

The training-only `request-label-codec.mjs` encodes already provided single-command `do`/`propose` graphs under the published `std.request` contract. Its validator imports the application's shared 0.192.0 bundle, pinned by the commit and hash in `src/vendor/sopworld/provenance.json`; no separate training bundle is retained. Sparse fields, tagged token/reference indices and explicit identity tables preserve original tokens, ordering, repeated occurrences and local references without turning zero/false/none into absence. Complete graphs must pass the actual published schema and request graph validator; unsupported question/compound profiles are refused.

Unresolved field diagnostics remain a separate envelope state, with provenance and candidates. They cannot be serialized as complete public request wires and carry no assertion that other graph relationships are valid. Resolution followed by complete validation is mandatory; removing the unresolved flag cannot bypass scope/selector checks. Size/index bounds fail closed without truncation. This is a lossless label representation and validation prerequisite, not approval or implementation of a learned head architecture.
