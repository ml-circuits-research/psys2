Measurement only (FORMALIZE-FROM-WORLDS, E1). Nothing in `src/` or `worlds/` was changed. Raw runs: `scripts/e1-collect.mjs` (rules, and the shipped `encoder+n1` through onnxruntime-node); classification: `scripts/e1-audit.mjs`, with the human decisions in `scripts/e1-overrides.json`. **Reading of the numbers (rules mode; encoder+n1 in the table).**

- English, where the errors are few (operational + dialogues, 131 non-right of 3537): 75.6% are c, d, e or h, 21.4% are a, b or f. Almost all of it is compound segmentation (h 42) and follow-ups or self-corrections (e 39); c is 18 (routing against the open tab or the only referent); missing words are only 9.
- English including the paraphrase gold and the real phrases (242 errors): 58.3% c+d+e+h, 35.5% a+b+f, 15 g. Here b is the largest single class (51, LLM paraphrases such as "which letters did i already try" that no pattern covers) and c is 34.
- Romanian operational (451 errors): 80% are a, b or f and only 18.2% c+d+e+h. 349 of the 451 have a right English source, so the meaning is already handled by the world: the translator route or generated Romanian patterns fix them, world-aware reading cannot (the translation is wrong or unread before any world looks at it). Of those, 125 have words left untranslated, 224 have an in-vocabulary translation the rules do not read.
- World-aware reading therefore pays in English, in compound messages and in follow-ups, not in Romanian. The encoder already removes most of the h class (8 left of 42 in the English core) and so the headroom for E2 on top of the shipped mode is mostly e and c: 43 + 15 of 99 English-core errors, plus the wrong answers (130 wrong only in encoder+n1, 62 of them in loose-words, 30 in typos, 21 in operational-ro, 12 in dialogues, 2 in operational) where a state check can veto a reading.

The English-sets row is dominated by the typo set (every typo error is class a: the typo is not a lexicon word) and the loose-words set (every non-right row is class e: intent to guess from a word salad). The per-set table below separates them.

## 8. How far to trust the classes

- **Mechanical, weak:** c from a contested routing runner-up or a dialogue topic; g from the gold record comparison (right world, ordinary answer, slot representation differs); e from anaphora/pronoun/short-turn markers (an "it" with only one referent in the state would be c; this is not tested here); d from "operation right, argument differs" and from reply wording that states a schema requirement.
- **By inspection:** the 18 operational cases in `scripts/e1-overrides.json` and the named cases of section 5.
- **By elimination:** b. It is the residue: no unknown word, no routing evidence, no follow-up marker. It is an upper bound for "no pattern covers it" and a lower bound for the others.
- **Not done:** an overlay check of candidate readings against the world instances (query/check in matrix mode) to reject readings the facts contradict. The state classes here rest on routing and tab evidence, not on a per-reading fact check; that check is the content of E2. The share of c is therefore a floor.
- The typo and loose-words sets are robustness probes, not user traffic; the unjudged real-phrase rows (log records with no expectation) are counted separately and are not errors here.
